Experts can't believe what the heck China just did to take the lead in AI. It wasn't their research capabilities or talent. It was something way more fundamental, their power grid. Here's what they found. China has been running their electrical grid with 80 to 100% reserve capacity for decades. That means they have double the power they currently need just sitting there ready to use. The U.S. runs at an embarrassing 15% reserve capacity, barely enough to keep the lights on. This changes everything about the AI race. In China, when they want to build a massive data center for AI training, the power is already there waiting. No permits, no delays, no problems. In America, companies are literally building their own private power plants because the grid can't handle the demand. Regular people are getting hit with higher electricity bills as AI infrastructure pushes local grids to their limit. One expert put it like this. China is set up to hit grand slams. China planned their energy infrastructure decades ahead, knowing they'd need it eventually. America only responds once the demand shows up. By then, it's too late. This isn't about who has better AI researchers or more funding. It's about who can actually power the technology at scale. When one country has unlimited energy ready to deploy and the other is scrambling to keep up with basic demand, the competition might already be decided.
