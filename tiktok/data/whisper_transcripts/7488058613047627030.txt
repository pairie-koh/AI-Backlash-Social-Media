Millions of people have watched this video on TikTok, but it's fake. It's one of several AI-generated fakes that have gone viral after the deadly earthquake in Myanmar. There's been plenty of footage of real devastation. So how do we know this video isn't real? We found this early version of the clip on TikTok and it has a watermark of an AI video generator. This clip alone had over 14 million views. Versions which cropped the watermark out spread across social media too. But even without a watermark, there are some telltale signs of AI generation. One expert has told us that one of the clearest signs of AI fakery in videos like these is people. Viewers should watch out for any unnatural or unrealistic moves, like these jerky movements of people here. And the video just doesn't look like the location it claims to be from. The footage is set to show the city of Mandalay. There are temples in the area that do look similar to the ones in the AI video, but nowhere can you see them laid out like this. This AI fake seems to to be a growing trend of engagement farming when there is breaking news. While they may not always cause harm in real life, fakes like this can add to confusion at a time when it's important to know what you can trust.
