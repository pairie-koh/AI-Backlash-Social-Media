The UK government have done a bit of a U-turn when it comes to AI and copyright policy thanks to artists like Dua Lipa and Sir Elton John. Initially the government said that AI companies could use copyrighted materials to train their AI models. A lot of artists have come forward to say that their books have been used to train AI models. Musicians like Georgia Smith are dealing with AI cloning basically their voice, their art and their style. Apps like Suno, Mozart and Eleven Labs allows people like you and me to create AI music in the style of other people and distribute it. So in June of last year the UK government passed the data use and access bill despite calls from artists like Dua Lipa and Elton John to make changes that required these AI companies to declare when they had used copyrighted material to train their models. The bill initially stated that AI companies could opt out of this declaration. Fast forward to last month and the technology secretary Liz Kendall said that they were now rethinking their positioning on this there's no definitive conclusion yet but this is a really good example of just how complex the impact of AI is UK government clearly wants to be ahead in this AI rate but it does mean that they have to make the decision between regulation and policy that not only ensures that AI is safe to use but provides protections for industries careers and culture or they go with the second option and follow a bit more of a lenient approach of providing the foundational policies and regulations and then letting the AI companies make the final decisions and let the AI chips fall where they may fall while we deal with the consequences. I'm hoping there's a middle ground but this might take years maybe even decades to get the balance right.
