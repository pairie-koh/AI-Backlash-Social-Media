Rule-based AI. A rule-based AI is pretty simple. It doesn't learn or adapt over time. Instead, it just follows a fixed set of instructions it was given from the start. This is why it's sometimes called a knowledge-based system. This type of AI works best for straightforward tasks with predictable results. For example, a thermostat turns on the heater when it senses the temperature is too low and an alarm clock rings at a set time. These kinds of devices don't change or learn. They just respond to certain triggers. Other examples are automatic doors that open when someone walks up to them or a calculator that does math for you. Context-based AI. Context-based AI is a smarter, more advanced type of artificial intelligence. Unlike simpler AIs that just follow basic rules, this kind of AI pays attention to the context. It looks at things like the surrounding environment, your behavior, past actions, and real-time cues to make decisions. For example, virtual assistants like Siri, Google Assistant, and Alexa don't just respond to simple commands. They consider past interactions, your preferences, and real-time info. So if you ask Siri for a restaurant, it might look at the time of day, your location, and places you've liked before. One big advantage of context-based AI is that it can make predictions. By learning from your data over time, it can guess what you might need or want in the future. For example, YouTube and TikTok use your viewing history to suggest videos you might enjoy, and we all know how good they've gotten at it. Narrow domain AI. Also called weak AI, this type of artificial intelligence is made to do one specific task really well. It's super focused on one job and often outperforms humans in that area, but it can't do anything outside of its specialty. Unlike general AI which can handle a wide range of tasks which we cover later down the line narrow AI can apply its skills to anything other than its specific purpose You can find examples of narrow AI across different fields In healthcare for example some AI systems are used to look at medical images like MRIs or X spotting early signs of diseases like cancer, and they're often more accurate than human doctors. Narrow AI can get so good that it can even beat world champions like AlphaGo, an AI that won against the top player in the ancient and complex board game Go. Another famous example is IBM's Deep Blue, which beat chess champion Garry Kasparov in 1997. We also use narrow AI in everyday things like spam filters in email and facial recognition on smartphones. As of now, the best AIs in the world are at this level. Reasoning AI Reasoning AI is a type of artificial intelligence that tries to think a bit more like humans do, making it closer to how we analyze and understand things. It looks at data, finds patterns, and makes logical conclusions, similar to how we reason and think. To create this kind of AI, developers use info from psychology to get closer to how human reasoning works. Many AI models today might seem like they're reasoning, but they're still considered narrow AI because they don't actually understand the world. For example, ChatGPT can answer complicated questions and hold conversations, which feels like reasoning, but it's really just following patterns and data, not truly understanding or being aware. Reasoning AI relies on huge amounts of data and complex algorithms to handle tasks that need a lot of decision-making. A good example is self-driving cars. They don't just see obstacles, they also analyze things like the speed and direction of nearby cars to make safe choices in real time. These AIs are trained on millions of driving scenarios to develop a kind of intuition for driving. That's a bit like how humans make decisions on the road.
