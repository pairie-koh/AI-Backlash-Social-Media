The Andromeda galaxy looks close in the night sky, but the numbers will blow your mind. Andromeda sits 2.5 million light-years away. That's 24 quintillion kilometres. If you drove a car at 100 kilometres per hour, you'd arrive in 28 trillion years. That's more than 2,000 times the current age of the universe. A plane at 900 kilometres per hour, still 3 trillion years. The fastest spacecraft ever built, the Parker Solar Probe, hits 700,000 kilometres per hour. At that record-breaking speed, Andromeda is still 4 billion years away, so the nearest galaxy isn't near at all. It's so far that no machine we've ever made could reach it within the lifetime of our species. And yet, in 4.5 billion years, Andromeda is on a collision course with the Milky Way, turning the night sky into something truly cosmic. .
