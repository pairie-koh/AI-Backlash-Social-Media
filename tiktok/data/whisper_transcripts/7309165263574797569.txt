The copyright case against AI art generators just got stronger with more artists and evidence. In the ongoing legal battle against AI art generators like Mid Journey and DeviantArt, artists pressing charges have strengthened their case with seven new plaintiffs, including notable names like Grzygors Rutkowski and Jingna Zhang. The amended complaint brings forth compelling evidence, asserting that even non-copyrighted works may be protected if they bear the artist's distinctive mark. Additionally, it highlights the potential unauthorized copying of copyrighted works during AI model training, raising concerns about the ethical use of data sets. The lawsuit emphasizes the fundamental design of diffusion models, aiming to replicate a training image accurately, supported by research papers from Google DeepMind and MIT. With crucial questions about the nature of AI-generated output yet to be ensuared, the court's decision could have far-reaching implications for the intersection of AI technology and copyright law.
