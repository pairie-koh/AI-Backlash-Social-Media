Meta just announced plans to slash 10% of its entire workforce next month. Chief People Officer Janelle Gale said in a memo that the layoff, which is scheduled for May 20th, is being done to run the company more efficiently and to offset other investments. It's an unusual move to announce layoffs a month in advance, and the real fallout may be the month-long limbo before they hit. Inside the company, workers are bracing for brutal weeks ahead. On forums like Blind, workers are responding to the news with a mix of questions, concerns, and dark jokes. One post was titled 28 Days of Hell. Another asked, how are you motivating yourself to work for the next month, with layoffs confirmed? For some, the company's acknowledgement of layoffs brought some relief. One worker said that the cuts were being so widely discussed internally that the announcement brought some ease of uncertainty. Another worker told Business Insider that the announcement of cuts added pressure for them to deliver results over the next month. Meta employs more than 78,000 people worldwide, so the move will affect approximately 7,800 of them. While Meta didn't specify which investments it's offsetting with the layoffs, the tech giant is spending hundreds of billions of dollars on AI, splashing it on huge data centers and top AI researcher compensation packages. So the big takeaway? Meta workers are bracing for a month of uncertainty. Some may spend it business as usual, and others may cut their losses now and start applying elsewhere.
