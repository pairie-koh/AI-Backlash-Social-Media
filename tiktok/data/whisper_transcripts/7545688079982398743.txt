Why do clouds float? It sounds impossible, right? How can billions of tonnes of water just hang in the sky without falling straight down? Here's the science. Clouds are made of tiny water droplets and ice crystals, so small and so light that warm air rising from the ground keeps them suspended. Warm air is less dense than cold air, so it pushes upward, lifting the cloud particles with it. Even though a single cloud can hold millions of litres of water, those droplets are microscopic, each one about 100 times thinner than a human hair. But when droplets collide and grow heavier, the air can't hold them anymore. And that's when they fall as rain. Here's the crazy part. Some clouds weigh as much as 500,000 kilograms. That's the weight of 100 elephants floating above your head right now. So clouds float because physics balances gravity, pulling them down with warm air pushing them up. Every time you look up at the sky, you're watching a delicate battle between weight and air. And somehow, gravity lets the clouds win.
