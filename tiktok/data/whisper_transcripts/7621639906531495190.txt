OpenAI is shutting down its AI video making app Sora. But why? Sora made headlines at the tail end of 2024 when it was released, being able to generate short video clips after just being given a text prompt. But there are a few reasons why it's been canned. The first, and this isn't surprising at all, is money. According to market experts, since the platform's launch, Sora has made $1.4 million in global net in-app revenues, compared to 1.9 billion over the same period for chat GPT. So financially, Sora was a bit of a black hole, sucking up resources with little monetary return. Second, disinformation. There's been a substantial rise in fake news and AI slops spreading as AI generation tools become more publicly available. Back in 2020, the number of media reported AI content incidents was 47. Now, nearly 6 years later, it's gone up almost tenfold to 475. Despite including a watermark to prevent misuse and the spread of misinformation, third-party apps were soon able to remove it. So that didn't help things. Third and finally, copyright. So Sora 2, the next iteration of Sora, originally had an opt-out stance where companies would have to request to have their intellectual property, things like characters and settings, moved. Following backlash and risking potential lawsuits, it was changed to opt in. A few months later, Disney became the first company to license its characters to OpenAI's video tools in a $1 billion deal, so you could generate your own little Grogu. And now, with the announcement of Sora closing down, that deal has been cancelled. So, with many challenges and issues in the way, and barely any money made to make it worthwhile, it isn't a shock to see OpenAI close down Sora. Does this mean we're seeing a sign that the AI bubble will pop?
