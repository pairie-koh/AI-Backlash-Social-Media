It's unsanctioned political graffiti, but they use light instead of paint. An anonymous LA-based collective called VJ Bombs are filmmakers who use high-powered projectors to turn city walls into canvases for dissent. They create guerrilla projections as a form of peaceful protest, calling them accessible, disruptive, but not violent. The work is confrontational, sometimes even grotesque, and sometimes hauntingly beautiful, such as the image of the Statue of Liberty slowly sinking. In an interview, they said, the sweet spot for us is when architecture and message intersect, when the building itself becomes a part of the story we're telling. This image was projected on the Twin Towers Jail in LA. Their work is projected without permission or forewarning. Since it's projected from public spaces, sometimes a car or a nearby lot, and it's certainly not permanent and doesn't cause any damage, there's not much the building owner can do. By using light instead of paint, they can take over a massive space, get tons of visibility, make a strong point, and then disappear before the police arrive.
