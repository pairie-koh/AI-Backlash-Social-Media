What does it take to become an architect? Age 18. You graduate high school. You decide you want to design buildings. You've never drafted a blueprint before, but one day you'll be responsible for structures that hundreds of people live and work inside. Salary, $0. Age 22. Architecture school. Design theory. Structural engineering. Technical drawing. You pull your first all-nighter building a scale model out of cardboard and glue. It collapses the morning before the review. You rebuild it overnight. Salary, $0. Age 24. You graduate with a degree and a portfolio. Now comes the part nobody warned you about. Three years of internship hours required before you can even sit the licensing exam. Salary, $42,000. Age 27. Architectural intern at a mid-sized firm. You spend most of your time on construction documents, site plans, and fixing other people's details. It's not glamorous, but you're learning how buildings actually get built. Salary, $58,000. Age 30. Licensed architect. You passed all nine divisions of the exam. Clients finally trust you with your own projects. Small ones, but yours. Salary, $78,000. Age 38 Senior architect You're leading projects now Managing teams Presenting to clients Making decisions that end up in concrete and steel Salary $115,000 Age 47 Principal or partner at a firm Your name is attached to buildings that will stand long after you're gone Salary $180,000 Not bad for someone who once rebuilt a cardboard model at 3 a.m.
