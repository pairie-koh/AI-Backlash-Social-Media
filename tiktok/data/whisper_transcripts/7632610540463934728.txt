This is crazy breaking news in South Africa. South Africa's government wrote a national policy on AI. They used AI to write it, and the AI made up the sources. This is genuinely one of the most ironic news stories I've read in years. News24 just broke this exclusively, and the receipts are devastating. Just a quick setup. Earlier this month, South Africa's communications and digital technologies minister, Solly Malachi, he published the country's draft national AI policy in the Government Gazette. It proposes creating an AI ethics board, an AI safety institute, and even an AI insurance super fund to compensate people harmed by AI. This is a big, serious policy. Then News24 reviewed the 67 references at the bottom of the document. At least six of them are completely fake. The South African Journal of Philosophy is real, but the article cited in the policy is not. The journal's managing editor, Dominique Griffiths, told News24, and I'm quoting, the reference is definitely an AI hallucination. The Journal of Ethics and Social Philosophy's managing editor Louise Simpson confirmed her journal never published the article either. The editor-in-chief of AI and Society said he can't locate the article in his own journal. And one citation references a journal called the AI Policy Journal that, as far as anyone can tell, just does not exist. A Stellenbosch professor named Johan Furie even ran the citation through an AI tool. The AI confirmed they weren't found in any academic database. In short, they were made up. Now, the department's response is the cherry on top here. They called these minor referencing discrepancies and said it doesn't affect the substance, integrity, or policy direction of the policy. A government policy on responsible AI use was written using AI irresponsibly. And the official response is that it doesn't matter. If your AI policy cannot pass an AI policy fact check, then maybe it's time to start over.
