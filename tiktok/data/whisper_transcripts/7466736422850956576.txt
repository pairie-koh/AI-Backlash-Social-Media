The EU AI Act is the world's first comprehensive legal framework setting out rules for what AI can and cannot do. It impacts everyone in Europe and businesses which operate there. Now, most of it won't actually apply until 2026 in order to give businesses and government the time to prepare. But there are two really important rules that kick in today, the 2nd of February 2025. The first of which is Article 4, AI literacy. Basically, businesses that make, use or sell AI have to ensure their relevant employees understand the impact and the risk. The second is Article 5. This prohibits the use of AI for cases that undermine our fundamental rights, like AI that manipulates or deceives, AI that exploits vulnerabilities like age or economic insecurity, AI that performs social scoring, basically ranking based on behaviour, like a credit score, but a lot more invasive. no AI that scrapes faces and images from the internet without permission no AI that predicts criminal behaviour based on profiling no AI that analyses emotions in the workplace or schools and finally but not exhaustively no AI that performs real-time biometric surveillance in public places now there are some exceptions to this for example in a case of imminent threat to security or kidnapping But by August 2025, businesses that breach Article 5 could have to pay a fine of up to €35 million or 7% of their annual global revenue.
