When investors stop asking, does this make money, and start asking, does this have AI in the name? you're in dangerous territory. Google, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, and Apple can afford to burn billions on AI development because whoever gets it right first might dominate the next era of computing. But here's where it gets interesting. Unlike past bubbles, the underlying technology actually works. AI is already saving radiologists time by flagging potential tumors and scans. It's helping developers write code faster. It's optimizing supply chains and predicting equipment failures before they happen. These aren't hypothetical future benefits. They happening right now and generating real value Yes some AI startups will fail spectacularly Yes some of these billion valuations will look ridiculous in hindsight Yes there will be a correction when investors realize not every AI company is the next big thing. But they're not adopting it as fast as the investment money is flowing in. That gap is what creates bubble conditions. If adoption catches up, this becomes a revolution. If it doesn't, a lot of investors are going to lose a lot of money. The hype makes it genuinely hard to tell which AI products are solving real problems and which ones are just capitalizing on the moment. If the big companies win, AI stays centralized in the hands of a few giants. If the bubble pops and takes out the big players competition, you get even less choice. Not because it's fake, but because it was real enough to be believable and exciting enough to attract capital. The hype is real. The value is also real. The question is, which one wins. They'll be the companies actually solving problems that people are willing to pay for, bubble or no bubble. So whether this is a bubble or a revolution, your best move is to focus on whether AI tools genuinely make your work better, not whether they have the flashiest promises. Because at the end of the day, useful technology survives, regardless of what the stock market does. But it does mean some of the enthusiasm might be driven more by, we need to invest in something, then this specific company is a great bet. That distinction matters when you're deciding which AI tools to trust with your work. But if we're actually building genuine infrastructure during a hype cycle, you might end up with an explosion of tools and capabilities you can't even imagine right now.
