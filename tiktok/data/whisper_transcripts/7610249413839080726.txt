Every cell in your body carries a code older than civilisation. Inside you right now is DNA. A molecule so small you can't see it, yet powerful enough to build you from scratch. If you stretched the DNA from just one cell, it would be about two metres long. Now multiply that by the roughly 30 trillion cells in your body. That's enough DNA to reach the sun and back hundreds of times. Your DNA is written in just four letters. Four. And with that tiny alphabet, it stores more information than every book, movie and song humanity has ever created. You share about 99.9% of your DNA with every other human, that tiny fraction left over. That's your face, your voice, your quirks. You even share chunks of DNA with bananas, mice and ancient bacteria. Because life didn't start over. It copied, edited and kept going. And here's the wild part. Your DNA isn't a blueprint, it's a recipe. Same ingredients, different outcomes, which means your body is not fixed. It's constantly reading, rewriting and responding to the world around you.
