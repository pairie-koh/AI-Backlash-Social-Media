Archie Collingwood published The Principles of Art in 1938. His test is simple. Art means the creator discovers what they feel through the act of creating. They don't know the emotion until it comes out. Craft means following a plan toward a known output. Type A prompt into Suno. Lo-fi, hip-hop, minor key, sad, 90 BPM. and the system executes a probability distribution toward a predetermined style. Known input, known output. By Collingwood's framework, that's craft and not art. But when Margaret Bynum, the creator behind China Styles, 20 million Spotify streams, Billboard number two hit, fed her childhood diary entries about an abusive household in Desuno. She was discovering what she needed to say through the act of creating. That moment of emotional discovery is what Collingwood called art. The AI was the instrument, the expression was hers. The same applies to Olivia B. Moore, a 43-year-old mother of five who lost her IT job and turned to Suno to process depression, grief, and survival. Her music is a chronological healing journey. She had no product in mind. She was finding out what she felt by making something. Her track, Love Notes, has over 8 million streams. Here's what's interesting.
