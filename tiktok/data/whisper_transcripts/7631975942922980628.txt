Robots today are mostly driven by cylindrical servo motors, but biological systems move with compliant fiber-like muscles. In this project, we developed electrofluidic fiber muscles, a new type of soft artificial muscle. They combine an antagonistic pair of thin fluidic actuators with thin, integrated electrohydrodynamic fiber pumps, allowing the pressure and motion to be generated directly from electrical input without bulky or external pumps or reservoirs. This enables untethered operation in a compact fiber form. Each muscle weighs only about 2 grams, is compliant and silent, and can be packaged easily into tight spaces. We also show that by changing how the pumps and actuators are arranged, we can tune performance for different robotic tasks. tasks. Arranging muscles in bundles scales up force, lifting loads of several kilograms. Using more pumps in parallel leads to faster response. We show a level arm that launches objects in just 100 milliseconds. By combining skeletal muscle level power density with a highly modular fiber-like form factor, we believe electrofluidic fiber muscles can become a powerful new building block for untethered robots, wearables and assistive technologies.
