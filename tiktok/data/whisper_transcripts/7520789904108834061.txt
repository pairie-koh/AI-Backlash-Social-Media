Is AI going to kill the legitimacy of the Internet? Yeah, it might. Or at least, it's already warping it. Here's the uncomfortable truth. AI is flooding the Internet with content faster than humans can verify, vet, or even react to. That has huge consequences for legitimacy, credibility, and trust online. 1. Information overload. Credibility collapse. AI can produce infinite articles, reviews, comments, images, even fake citations. So search engines, social media, and forums are drowning in content. But when anyone can generate expert text in seconds, the value of actual expertise starts to erode. You get SEO spam instead of useful answers, fake reviews drowning out real ones, Synthetic news that sounds accurate, but has no grounding. The death of source tracing. Once AI content starts feeding back into itself, AI trained on AI, you get a loop of distorted, contextless information. We're already seeing this with chat GPT-written blogs being scraped and fed into newer models, or citations being hallucinated and passed around like they real That creates a polluted ecosystem like trying to drink water downstream from a factory dumping waste into the river 3 Visual and audio deepfakes It's no longer just about text. These voices, faces, and entire videos can now be faked. That makes seeing or hearing something no longer a guarantee of its authenticity. It chips away at one of the last bastions of proof we had online. 4. Trust gets decentralized or destroyed. Ironically, people may start trusting private, closed communities more than the open web, because there's some sense of human curation, even if biased. Or they'll rely more on platforms with strong identity verification and moderation, which also opens up dystopian possibilities. So is the Internet dead? Not dead, but it's undergoing a legitimacy crisis. We're moving from, if it's online, it must be true, to, if it's online, it might be AI, fake, spam, or unverified. What replaces legitimacy will depend on how people adapt, through new tech, norms, or even going back to older ways of verification, like real-life reputation or human consensus.
