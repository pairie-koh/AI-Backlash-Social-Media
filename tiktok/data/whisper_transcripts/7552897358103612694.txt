Here's a fact that sounds impossible. A single tree is rarer in the universe than pure gold. Gold is forged in the hearts of dying stars and scattered across galaxies during supernova explosions. That's why astronomers keep finding it in asteroids, in cosmic dust, even in the atmospheres of distant planets. But a tree? That requires something almost no other place in the universe has. A stable planet, at the perfect distance from its star, with liquid water, the right atmosphere and billions of years of evolution. Our galaxy alone has hundreds of billions of stars and nearly all of them contain traces of gold. But when it comes to something as simple as an oak tree, out of trillions of planets, Earth might be the only one carrying them. So the rarest treasure in the cosmos isn't gold.
