The artificial intelligence boom is a physical, heavy industry. Training a large language model requires operating thousands of graphics processing units, at a feverish case, converting electricity into heat and computation. Unlike a factory that spins down at night, these hyperscaling data centers draw power 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. The energy demand is continuous and massive. The hardware driving this demand relies on extreme density. Thousands of server racks are packed into single facilities, drawing concentrated loads that existing local grids were never engineered to support. At the exact moment this massive continuous load is scaling up, regional power grids are adding record amounts of intermittent wind and solar. Generation fluctuates wildly, while data center demand remains perfectly flat. This mismatch exposes a severe bottleneck. The physical transmission lines and power plants that make up the national grid are struggling to balance the chaotic output of renewables against the rigid demands of the data economy.
