A court found that ChatGPT can generate accurate summaries of books by famous authors continuing a case of copyright infringement against OpenAI. On October 28th, U.S. District Judge Sidney Stein, a federal judge in New York, is allowing plaintiffs' claim to proceed on copyright infringement caused by OpenAI. The central claim that authors' copyrights were infringed when OpenAI used their works to train its artificial intelligence systems was not dismissed. OpenAI and other tech companies argue that using copyrighted material for AI training is permissible under the fair use provision of U.S. copyright law. Multiple authors, including Game of Thrones creator George R.R. Martin, have joined the New York lawsuit against OpenAI. The judge rejected OpenAI's argument that no reasonable jury could conclude its AI-generated outputs were substantially similar to the author's original works and therefore did not infringe on their copyrights. Judge Stein cited a ChatGPT-generated summary of Martin's Game of Thrones. In the court case, it states, When prompted, ChatGPT generated an infringing, unauthorized, and detailed outline for an alternate sequel to A Clash of Kings. One of the Martin-infringed works entitled the infringing and unauthorized derivative A Dance with Shadows, using the same characters from Martin's existing books in the series A Song of Ice and Fire.
