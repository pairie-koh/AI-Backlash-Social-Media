Picture this. The CEO of the most hyped tech company on earth just admitted his $200 per month subscription, loses money on every single customer, not a little money, not break-even with growth potential, loses money. And Wall Street's response? They valued his company at $500 billion anyway. Welcome to the AI bubble, where we went from cool chatbot to $7 trillion gamble in 24 months, and why the people writing the checks might be in for the rudest awakening since 2008. November 30th, 2022. Just a regular Wednesday. Unremarkable. Cold brew flowing. Engineers debugging code. Then OpenAI dropped ChatGPT. Within 48 hours, the internet had a new obsession. People were using it to write breakup texts, debug code, explain black holes, and generate bedtime stories. Dad, I'm talking to an AI that knows everything. It just explained quantum entanglement using Minecraft terms. Did I hear Minecraft? It has 1 million users in the first five days of launch. Two months later, 100 million users. Fastest growing app in history. Instagram took 2.5 years to hit 100 million. Facebook took 4.5 years. ChatGPT did it in 60 days. And Silicon Valley, absolute panic. Inside Google's headquarters, executives were having a meltdown. We invented this technology. The transformer model, that was us. The research paper has our names on it. Then why is my son using ChatGPT and not Google AI? Because we were too scared to release it. Well, release something now. Call it Bard, Gemini, whatever. Ship it. Google scrambled. They had the tech. They'd had it for years, but they were terrified of what it might say. Lawsuits? Of bad press Open AI had no such fears They shipped first And in tech first mover advantage is everything Within weeks Microsoft threw billion at OpenAI with another $3 billion on standby. Google panic launched Bard, which immediately hallucinated in its first demo. Amazon started dumping billions into Anthropic. We're a dog-walking app, but the root optimization uses machine learning? Say no more. Here's $50 million. Don't you want to see our demo? Shut up and take my money. By mid-2023, if your slide deck mentioned neural networks or large language models, you were basically printing money. Companies with zero revenue, no product, just a team and a Google Doc, were raising $100 million rounds. The bubble gave us technological marvels like AI-powered toothbrushes, AI recipe generators, AI dating coaches. One company raised $30 million to build an AI that summarizes potentially other AI outputs. We'd reached peak absurdity. But the real insanity? The GPU shortage? These little computer chips, which look like harmless circuit boards, became more valuable than gold or oil. NVIDIA became one of the most valuable companies on Earth. Their CEO, Jensen Huang, started wearing leather jackets to conferences like he was the Mick Jagger of semiconductors. And he kind of was. Renting a single top-tier H100 GPU costs about $3 an hour. Sounds cheap, right? But startups don't need one. They need hundreds. A small startup trying to train just one model might rent a 500 chip cluster. That's $1,500 per hour. $36,000 per day? Over $1.1 million per month just for renting GPU. Yet companies were still hoarding GPUs, like doomsday preppers stockpile canned beans. Nvidia couldn't manufacture them fast enough. The waitlist was, at one point, up to 12 months long? Black markets emerged? Seriously. People were smuggling $100 bills across borders.
