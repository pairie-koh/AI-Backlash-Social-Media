After fierce battles with meta and open AI, California is leading the way as usual and just passed the nation's first AI transparency law. This fills a gap that Congress has failed to address. Governor Newsom signed SB53 on Monday, making California the first state to require tech companies to publicly disclose safety and security protocols. What does this mean for tech companies? Well, first of all, companies that make over $500 million a year in revenue must assess and publicly disclose risks that their AI could pose. The law creates an incident reporting system for safety issues. It also includes whistleblower protection for AI workers. And the fines are up to $1 million per violation. What's interesting about the bill is this is actually Governor Newsom's second attempt to pass something like this. He actually vetoed legislation last year after it got major pushback. This narrower version actually worked with tech companies, so OpenAI and Meta didn't oppose it and Anthropic actually supported the bill. Some venture capitalists are concerned that it still creates a patchwork of regulations across states. But if you ask me, this should be the starting point that every other state can learn from. It sets a clear precedent for what other states can follow and doesn't have that much pushback from tech companies. What happens next? Well, Congress is debating whether they should create their own regulations. And if that happened, then these federal standards would override the state laws.
