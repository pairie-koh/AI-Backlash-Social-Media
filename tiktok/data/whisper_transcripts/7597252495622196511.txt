Artists are fighting back against Twitter's AI, and the way they're doing it is absolute genius. It all started when the platform introduced a new feature that very quickly became controversial, that of course being the edit image button. It was a button that was directly baked into every post containing an image on the platform, and allowed anybody to alter the image just by clicking it using AI. Obviously, this upset a lot of artists on the platform, because it literally allowed anybody with one click to alter their artwork with AI. However, artists very quickly realized how to beat the system. See, the edit image button actually only showed up on photo posts, so if you posted your art in a different type of file, like say a GIF, it actually wouldn't have the option to modify it with AI. And so the entirety of the artist community on Twitter unified and entered a renaissance of making GIFs. Some people genuinely made little cute animations that fit the gif format, and some people just exported their static images as gifs. What this also led to, though, was an era of jump scares getting hidden in artwork. Because people were getting conditioned to assume that static artwork was getting exported as gifs, but was still static artwork. And it was a lie. It was not static artwork. Twitter's AI stuff is always such a mess, but you can also always count on artists to find creative ways around it.
