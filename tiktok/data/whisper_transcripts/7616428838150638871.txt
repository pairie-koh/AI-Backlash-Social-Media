So more people are turning to AI to talk about their mental health. And in some ways that makes sense. These tools are instant, they're always available and they can feel anonymous. But we need to talk about the risks of using AI chatbots for mental health. For example, these tools are designed to agree with you and to mirror your language. And this can feel warm and comforting in some moments, but it can also be harmful because there's a risk that they'll repeat or validate any negative thoughts that you might share with them. Also, AI chatbots don't always give you information you can actually trust. Even though their responses can sound really confident, they can still be wrong or they can say things that just aren't appropriate. These tools also can't tell if you're in a crisis or if you need immediate attention. And that can be really risky. They can miss important emotional cues that a trained professional would pick up. And they won't know if you're unsafe or if you need help. The thing is, if you're going through a tough time, you deserve real empathy and genuine connection. Whether you're looking for mental health information that you can trust, or someone who can listen to you in the moment, or support for a mental health issue, there are really good alternatives out there instead of turning to AI.
