Yeah, plagiarism detectors don't work. There aren't issues with the technology. It's just that students are truly too smart for them. I have rarely ever seen students just like copy and paste something in from ChatGPT and submit their work. And when I have seen that, it's really funny because I have 15 year old boys out here writing like William Shakespeare. I swear one of these used the word betwixt. Anyway, most of the time what they're doing is pasting in from ChatGPT or Snapchat AI or whatever, and then spending like 10, 20 minutes changing certain words and phrases to escape detection. There's also something called a spinner that just does this for them. And in the most egregious example of this would be quicker if you just did your work, I've actually seen students use like another device, their phone or something, generate the plagiarized text, and then hand type it in to their Chromebook. I mean, if students use the same ingenuity they use for cheating on their actual writing assignments, they would be winning Pulitzer Prizes. So until the technologies evolve, we have to just be a creative. I think prevention is key and the handwritten assignments have been working really well for my students this year. The kids are smart. We gotta stay one step ahead of them.
