AI companies know that their tools are energy hungry, they're just not telling anyone how much. OpenAI, for example, says that each chat GPT query uses 0.34 watt hours of energy. That's a small number on paper, but experts say that it's impossible to actually evaluate without details. There's no explanation of what counts as a typical query, or whether that figure includes training, image generation, or even the energy needed to cool servers. A climate researcher at Hugging Face said that the number given by OpenAI was so big that Sam Altman could have pulled that out of his ass. To be fair, most companies are even less transparent. In May 2025, nearly all LLM usage came from models with no environmental disclosures at all. That leaves users and regulators relying on vague stats and unverified comparisons. For example, a really popularly repeated claim that ChatGPT uses 10 times more energy than a Google search started off as an offhand comment from the chairman of Alphabet. In the meantime, open source researchers are trying to fill in the gaps. A A recent study of 14 public models found big differences in energy use, with larger models burning up to 50% more power to complete the same tasks. But importantly, these tests were run on older GPUs. Newer hardware like NVIDIA's H100 draws even more power. And that's just on a model level. At the infrastructure level, the energy footprint is growing fast, driven in large part by AI usage. A McKinsey report projects that U.S. data center energy demand will quadruple by 2030. Now, these numbers only scratch the surface. They don't include training costs, model updates, or the physical footprint of data centers. But without standard reporting, there's no real way to understand what AI is costing the planet as its adoption accelerates.
