I hear a lot of pro-AI people say a lot of disingenuous things about artificial intelligence, but one of the most disingenuous things I hear people say is that the panic and the fear about AI today is exactly like what people felt in the 90s about the internet. And that is categorically untrue. I'm a Harvard-trained professor who studies the impact of AI on society, and I need to tell you that AI sentiment today does not mirror the sentiment that Americans held toward the internet in the 90s. It doesn't even mirror the sentiment that Americans held towards social media in the 2000s. In fact, it's the opposite. A 2025 Pew Research study found that 50% of Americans were more concerned about AI than they were excited about. In fact, only 10% of Americans were more excited than concerned. Compare that to a 1999 Pew Research study about Americans' attitudes towards the internet, and we see that 61 of Americans liked computers and the internet and only 6 of them disliked them In the 90s we were not seeing widespread protests about challenging the infrastructure of the internet in the way that we're seeing massive amounts of organizing around challenging the building of data centers in local municipalities. We can say the same thing about social media. Around 2008, everyone was excited about the democratization of creating and sharing media. There was widespread enthusiasm about social media. The vibes around AI today are very different than the vibes that were around the technological innovations of the past. So don't let anyone historically gaslight you into thinking anything different. AI poses significant challenges to every aspect of our society. And the only way that we are going to circumvent those challenges is by continuing to voice our concerns about the technology and continuing to push back. Yes, AI is here to stay. You cannot put the toothpaste back in the tube. But the way AI impacts all of us depends on how we challenge it. And it depends on all of us continuing to express what we don't want so that we can get the future that we do.
