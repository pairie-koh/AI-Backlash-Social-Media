It's been described as the ramblings of a supervillain, rage-baint, and even techno-fascism. I'm talking, of course, about Palantir's recently posted 22-point mini-manifesto. In it, the company presents a vision of the world defined by constant risk, endless geopolitical competition, and above all, fear. And in point five of their manifesto, they talk about AI weapons, the very thing we at StopKillerRobots have been campaigning against for over a decade. Palantir says, The question is not whether AI weapons will be built. It is who will build them and for what purpose. Our adversaries will not pause to indulge in theatrical debates about the merits of developing technologies with critical military and national security applications. They will proceed. For Palantir, the development and use of these technologies is framed as inevitable. And why? Because our adversaries are supposedly building them too. This is the logic of an arms race. Additionally, Palantir creates a sense of urgency designed to pressure states into sidestepping what they dismiss as theatrical debates. In other words, conversations about safety, legality, accountability, and the protection of civilians. Their message is clear. If states stop to consider the consequences of developing and deploying these technologies, they will fall behind. They will lose. But we heard this argument before AI is already being increasingly incorporated into warfare sold on the promise that it will help states win faster more efficiently and with fewer casualties Yet in conflicts where AI enabled tools have been used, like Ukraine, Gaza, and Iran, we have not seen faster resolutions or lasting peace. Instead, we've seen prolonged violence and the deaths of countless civilians and soldiers. And here's the key point. Palantir is not making a neutral observation. They're making a business case. They're making an argument for their own relevance, and they're making an argument for the necessity of the AI weapons they develop and sell. Technology can and should be used to promote peace, strengthen democracy, and empower people. Yet the technology Palantir is selling risks doing the opposite, oppressing, dehumanizing, and ****. But there is hope. These same so-called theatrical debates, the ones that could create legal safeguards around the development and deployment of autonomous weapons, are already advancing at the United Nations. And this November, states have an opportunity to launch negotiations on a treaty to stop killer robots. With companies like Palantir developing and marketing these technologies while rejecting safeguards, the need for regulation has never been more urgent.
