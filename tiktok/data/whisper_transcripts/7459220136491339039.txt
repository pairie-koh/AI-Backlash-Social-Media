This is Clearview AI. If you haven't heard of them, you're not alone. They like it that way. This company has quietly built one of the most invasive surveillance tools in human history, and they've done it by scraping over 30 billion images from the internet. Your social media accounts, your profile pictures, your posts, all without your consent. Think about that for a second. Your face, your identity, harvested and stored in their database to be sold to the highest bidder. Governments, law enforcement agencies, even private companies, they're all lining up to use Clearview's technology. In the Netherlands, Clearview AI was hit with a massive fine, 30.5 million euros, for creating what regulators called an illegal database. That's right, illegal. The Dutch government didn't just fine them. They outright banned Dutch organizations from using their service, calling it a violation of privacy laws. And that's not the only legal trouble they've faced. In Illinois, Clearview settled a lawsuit for over $50 million for violating the state's privacy laws. Why? Because they collected and sold people's facial images without their consent. Sound familiar? And yet, despite all of this, law enforcement agencies are still using Clearview. The New York Police Department was caught using their tools, and even more concerning, some officers used it without authorization.
