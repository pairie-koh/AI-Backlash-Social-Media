Sometimes people ask me why as an artist am I pro AI? I wasn't always pro AI, I actually used to really hate it. I got a degree in game development, which I wouldn't recommend you do, but in one of our classes for experimental game design, they want us to experiment by using AI. And my game was basically this kind of like artsy game about why AI is bad. And to do that, I had to do a lot of thinking about why I really hated AI, because every time I saw it, I'd be like, this is an abomination, this is unholy, this is ungodly, why does this exist? We need to get rid of this. But after introspecting for a long time, I realized the reason I didn't like it was because it was a threat to my livelihood. As an artist, if that can get automated, then am I useful? Am I useful anymore? The truth is, art is a valuable endeavor in and of itself. It doesn't matter if you live in a cave and you're just drawing all day and nobody ever sees your art. The fact that you develop that skill is meaningful. Additionally, AI is far better at filling in the gaps that humans make than actually making stuff on its own. So as an artist, you'll still have creative vision and you'll still be able to do a lot of stuff. and knowing the underlying fundamentals will still matter. However, in the future, you're going to be able to color and shade using AI. Or you could show it a bunch of drawings that you've already done, and it can make it a lot faster to draw new drawings. Let's take something time-intensive like animation. Theoretically, if AI art gets good enough, you can create an animated sitcom on your own, just as a solo person. And that could be incredible. There's a lot of soulless AI art going around, but the reason it's soulless is because there's no artist behind it. If you're an artist, you know how to imbue meaning into something. That is the skill of an artist. I made a whole video on this and how it intersects with my understanding of computer science because I'm a software engineer. And I think once AI tools start getting good and useful, then we're gonna really see artists starting to jump over. Welcome to the digital era.
