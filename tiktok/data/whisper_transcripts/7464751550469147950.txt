Let's learn about generative AI and what makes it different. So you might have heard the term generative being used to describe different types of models recently like large language models, LLMs, and if you want to learn a little bit about how those work, check out my channel, I have a video on it, or image generation models like DALI. The reason the word generative is used for these models is because these models create new outputs in the sense that they're creating a new image or a new word or a new sample based on the samples it's received. So an example of a model that isn't generative is for example a computer vision problem where we are detecting when a light turns green. That's not generative. It's basically saying is it green or is it not green and at what time does it turn green. Another example would be a classification of a letter into one of the 26 English letters. That's a classification problem where a new letter isn't created based on how 26 letters looked before it. That would be generative. What generative models do is they learn the distribution of the data they've received and create new outputs according to that distribution.
