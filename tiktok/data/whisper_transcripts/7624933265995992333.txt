There's a complaint spreading across finance teams right now that ChatGPT has gotten worse, and I want to give you an honest take on what's actually happening, because some of it is real, and some of it is something else entirely. The something else is this. When ChatGPT first came out, most people were using it for simple tasks. Summarize this, rewrite this, explain this concept, and it was impressive because the bar was low. It was new. We weren't asking much of it. Now people are using it for actual finance work, complex modeling, scenario outputs, executive communication. The bar has gone up. The tool didn't necessarily get worse. The asks got harder. Now that said, there are real and documented changes to how these models behave over time. The last two OpenAI models have really honestly been annoying, overly complimentary, and just not getting to it. OpenAI keeps adjusting to chat GPT, and some of that has made it more cautious, more verbose, and in some cases, less precise on analytical tasks. And if you felt that, well, you're not imagining it. What I found in practice is that the output quality gap between a well-structured prompt and a lazy prompt has gotten wider, not smaller as we expected. The people getting great results are being more specific about the format they want, the constraints they want applied, and the role they want the tool to play. The people getting mediocre results are running the same vague prompts they used 18 months ago and wondering why the output isn't as impressive. The tool is still capable, but it rewards people who treat prompting like a skill, not a search bar. If your results have gotten worse, the first place is to look to the prompt, not the model.
