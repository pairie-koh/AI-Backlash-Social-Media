From babies trapped in space to cats with ginormous muscles, a wave of bizarre AI-generated videos are some of the most popular on YouTube right now. Nearly 1 in 10 of the fastest-growing YouTube channels globally are only publishing AI content, according to The Guardian. And this AI slop is hooking people in with the channels racking up millions of subscribers. YouTube is trying to stop the deluge of AI content making its way onto the platform with AI policies that block advertising revenue for channels that post inauthentic content. But it's not just YouTube facing this problem. Instagram, TikTok, Spotify, and Pinterest are becoming dumping grounds for AI slop. And experts in this space warn that as AI floods the zone, it could drive away real people and create fake low-quality environments where online communities can't grow or even survive. Instagram and TikTok say they require all realistic AI content to be labeled, but this isn't stopping it from spreading on the platforms. As AI tools become more powerful and the videos more bizarre, unless these platforms respond to actually stopping AI slop, you can probably expect even weirder content showing up on your feed.
