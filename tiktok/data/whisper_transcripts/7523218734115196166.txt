Your Netflix suggestions, map reroutes, or phone unlock using your face? Well, they're all powered by AI. And like it or not, AI has become an intrinsic part of our daily lives. But at what cost? AI refers to computer systems designed to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence. Unlike traditional software following predetermined instructions, AI systems learn from data, identify patterns, and make decisions with varying degrees of autonomy. But to make all of this work, there is a massive energy demand on physical data centers operate 24-7, consume electricity equivalents to entire cities, and require billions of gallons of water for cooling. It's estimated that if all data centre projects currently under regulatory review in Canada proceed, they could account for a staggering 14% of Canada's total power needs by 2030.
