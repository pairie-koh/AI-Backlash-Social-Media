A human walked into a room full of elite alien predators and walked out alone. The briefing called it a negotiation. Everyone in the room knew it wasn't. The Obsidian Table, a neutral meeting space carved into the bedrock of Vael Station, had hosted exactly 117 negotiations in its history. 93 of them had ended with one party leaving and one party not leaving in any meaningful sense. Ambassador Dara Mensah had read the historical record. She walked in anyway. There were seven of them. Representatives of the Convergence, the galaxy's most feared alliance of predator-class species, Vreth, Kalthari, a Syrian deep-caste, three others whose species names translated, in their own languages, to variations of apex and final and end. They had requested this meeting specifically because their intelligence suggested humanity was the one coalition species worth intimidating. Properly before the upcoming territorial vote Establish the hierarchy early Make the new species understand their position The room was designed for exactly this purpose Low lighting Oppressive acoustics Seven very large beings arranged in a semicircle facing a single chair. Dara sat in the single chair, crossed her, legs, put her coffee on the table. The Vreth representative spoke first. He was six feet at the shoulder even seated with the kind of physical presence that reorganized the air around it. You understand what this table is, he said. Not a question. I. Read the history, yes, Dara said. You came anyway. You asked me to. He leaned forward. We asked to see if you would. Dara picked up her coffee. And? And you walked in alone. No security, no diplomatic escort, No weapons we can detect. His eyes tracked her with the precise attention of something calibrated for hunting. Are you not afraid? Dara considered the question with genuine thoughtfulness Of what specifically she asked The Kaelthari representative mandibles clicked the sound of something recalibrating Of us, the Vreth said simply. Dara looked around the table. Seven species, seven sets of eyes built for tracking prey, seven beings who had walked into this room fully expecting a particular outcome. I'm sitting in a room with seven apex predators, she said. If I was going to be afraid, the time for that was before I sat down, she set her coffee on the table. But I made my decision in the hallway, so now I'm just having a conversation. Silence The Assyrian, deep cast, rarely spoke. In mixed company, tilted its head. You decided not to be afraid. I decided fear wasn't useful here, Dara said. It wouldn't change the room. It would just make me less effective in it. The meeting lasted 40 minutes Nobody made threats Not because the Convergence had abandoned the plan but because somewhere in the first 10 minutes the plan had stopped making sense You cannot establish hierarchy over something that has already decided where it stands. Dara hadn't challenged them, hadn't performed bravery, hadn't tried to match their physical presence with anything. She had simply been entirely, completely, unbudgingly herself, and that, the convergence found, was harder to work around than any weapon. One by one, they found reasons to conclude early. The Vreth representative was last. At the door, he paused. You are a strange species, he said. We get that a lot, Dara replied. He left. Dara finished her coffee, alone in a room that had seen 93 one-sided endings.
