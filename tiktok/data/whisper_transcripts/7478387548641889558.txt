Eight AI systems are now officially banned in the EU. As of February 2, the EU has banned certain uses of AI, posing them as unacceptable risk under the new AI Act. Why? Because these AI systems might be violating fundamental rights and they're not aligned with EU values. Let me explain. Social scoring. AI giving people a trustworthiness score based on their actions or data that could affect their access to jobs, loans or credit. AI that predict or assess the risk of an individual committing a crime. Creating facial recognition databases by untargeted scraping of the internet and CCTV footage. AI systems that recognize emotions in the workplace or education. AI systems that categorize people based on biometric data to deduce sensitive information like race, religion or political alignment. AI that purposefully manipulates or distorts behavior or that might cause significant harm. AI that exploits people's vulnerabilities based on their age, disabilities, race, or economic situation. The use of real-time remote biometric identification systems in public spaces for law enforcement. That's banned, with some limited exceptions. So the EU is pioneering AI regulation, and this poses some serious questions. On one hand, some might think that this might stifle tech innovation in Europe, making it harder for startups and companies to compete. On the other hand, some AI leaders argue that regulation on AI is as important as it ever was because this technology could have a deep impact in our societies, in our economies, and our democracies.
