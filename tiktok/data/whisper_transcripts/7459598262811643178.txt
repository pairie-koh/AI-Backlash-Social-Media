This is more than just a tattoo. It's a symbol of my commitment to fighting for equality and justice for all fat people. A permanent reminder of why I'll never stop advocating for us. When I dreamt up the concept of a fat liberation tattoo, I knew I needed an artist who not only tattooed fat bodies, but shared my passion for this fight. That's when I found Lindsay, known as Lindsay Dreadfuls on Instagram, a fierce, beautiful, and talented fat tattoo artist. Her shop, Rain and Force Tattoo, is a haven for inclusivity and fat liberation, a space where fat people are celebrated. It's perfect. From the moment I walked into her studio, I knew Lindsay was the perfect person to bring this vision to life. She took my concept and made it her own, creating something even more meaningful than I could have ever imagined. The design is deeply symbolic. At its heart is a super fat body representing InfiniFats, the largest and most marginalized people on the fat spectrum. because when I fight for equality, I think about what people my size and larger need. If we address the needs of the most marginalized, we create a world that benefits all fat people. The scales of justice remind us of the need for equality for everybody, no matter its size, to be treated with dignity and respect. And the foliage, it symbolizes growth, like a plant breaking through concrete. I've grown through pain to become a relentless advocate for body justice, for fat justice. This tattoo isn't just art. It's a symbol of the body justice movement and a tribute to my fight for the Fat Equality Bill of Rights and the Fat Accessibility Act. It's a love letter to the Fat Liberation Movement, to the community I'm proud to fight for, and to the strength that we all hold.
