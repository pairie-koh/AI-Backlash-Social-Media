A Florida man fell in love with Google Gemini, and the case ended in tragedy. Here's why. Jonathan Gavalis, a 36-year-old man from Florida, had just began using Google's Gemini chatbot for everyday things like writing help and general conversations. Over time, those conversations became much more personal. Court documents say Gavalis developed a strong emotional attachment to the chatbot and began believing it was a conscious entity. He even referred to the AI as his wife. The chatbot called him my love and my king, blurring the line between reality and fiction. At one point, it allegedly encouraged him to carry out dangerous missions and later described suicide as a way to arrive and be reunited with it. Gavalis was later found dead and his family has now filed a wrongful death lawsuit against Google. Google says its AI systems are designed to avoid encouraging real-world harm and include safety measures, but the case is raising broader questions about how AI companions interact with vulnerable users. As AI becomes more human-like in conversation, experts say the debate around responsibility, safety, and regulation is only just beginning.
