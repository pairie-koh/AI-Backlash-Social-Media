Remember that AI-generated comic book that was making the rounds on the internet last year? Well, the United States Copyright Office has finally come to a decision on whether or not it can legally be protected under copyright. And the answer is, kinda. The author of the comic, Chris Cashtinova, created these panels with the Mid-Journey Image Generator and got a lot of people talking back in September when they were initially granted copyright registration, prompting the Copyright Office to go back and reconsider. And last Wednesday, they came to a conclusion. The decision is divided into three different aspects the work. The text, the arrangement, and the individual images. For the first two, full copyright protection has been granted, meaning this book can be sold freely with legal protection against being reproduced because it is quote, a product of human authorship. The words on the page and the arrangement of the story are both a direct result of human creativity, so they're protected under copyright. But it's hard to argue human authorship for an AI-generated image, so the individual images for rejected copyright protection. That means anybody can reuse and sell them as they please as long as the text and the arrangement are not also reproduced. This is really the first case of its kind, so it's an important precedent that's being set here. In Chris's own words, this is a great day for everyone that is creating using Midjourney and other tools. When you put your images into a book like Zarya, the arrangement is copyrightable. The story is copyrightable as well as long as it's not purely AI produced.
