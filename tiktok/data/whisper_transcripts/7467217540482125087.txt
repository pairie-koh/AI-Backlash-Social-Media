Artificial intelligence is set to revolutionize the world of news. Deepfakes and generative models have already had an explosive impact on journalism. But can the industry survive this? For example, the war in Gaza has shown that AI has been used to manipulate and fabricate stories, making it harder to distinguish fact from fiction. Images were generated by AI portraying Israel in a positive light when these events didn't actually happen. AI alone doesn't create these narratives. AI systems are shaped by algorithms created by teams of research engineers and data scientists. Humans who bring their own biases with them. This raises a key question. Who really controls the AI narrative? One that can be distorted, selectively amplifying some voices while silencing others. One example is when users asked ChadGBT whether Israelis deserve freedom. ChadGBT simply said yes, but when it came to Palestinians, the answer was complex. In war zones like Gaza, this is especially dangerous. These tools are designed to help us make sense of the world, supposedly in a neutral way. But with flawed humans at the forefront, one can only assume that the technology will carry some of these flaws. So we have to ask, whose history might AI protect? And whose might it erase?
