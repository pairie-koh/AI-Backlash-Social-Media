Data centers in Australia have largely been flying under the radar, but now one of the world's biggest could be coming to Sydney. A Western Sydney site the size of 52 rugby fields has been earmarked for a US-owned 1-gigawatt data center. It would make existing ones look tiny. Powering this new one would require 2.8 million solar panels, or almost half the output from Victoria's largest coal power plant. The water needed to cool it could be the equivalent usage of 80,000 households, and its planned diesel backup generators will likely cause noxious air pollution. mega data centers in the US have caused serious problems for local communities, with electricity prices rising as much as 267% surrounding them. Besides the environmental and community concerns, there are signs that AI hype is slowing down. Major AI companies are already losing billions of dollars each year, and their examples of newly built mega data centers are sitting idle because of energy supply constraints. But with both the federal government and the private sector looking to position Australia as a regional data center hub, Even if this plan fails, we can expect mega data centers to be built here soon.
