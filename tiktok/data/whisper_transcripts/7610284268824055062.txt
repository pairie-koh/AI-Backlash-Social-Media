Salt is one of the strangest substances on Earth. It looks boring, but without it, you die. Every nerve signal in your body runs on salt. Your thoughts, your movement, your heartbeat, all depend on charged sodium ions flowing at the right moment. Salt once decided who was rich and who starved. Roman soldiers were paid in it. That's where the word salary comes from. There are places on Earth where salt falls from the sky. In deserts, ancient oceans evaporated and left behind layers so thick they can bend the Earth's crust. Salt is older than bones, older than trees, older than most life. Some salt deposits formed hundreds of millions of years before the first dinosaurs existed. Your body craves salt not because it tastes good, but because evolution wired you to hunt it relentlessly. And here's the wild part. Salt doesn't expire. The salt you sprinkle today could be the same salt that once sat at the bottom of an ancient sea. Which means every grain carries a memory of a planet that no longer exists.
