Social media users are sharing these AI-generated images, falsely claiming that they're from security camera footage of Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky with convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein. In the pictures, we can see a figure resembling Zelensky shaking hands and talking with Epstein, with the captions alleging that the two were close and met each other in intimate, non-public settings on the disgraced financier's private island. However, there's plenty of evidence to prove that the images are fake. Firstly, we ran them through Google's AI platform Gemini, which detected a synth ID on the image. This is an invisible watermark developed by Google, which shows that all or part of the content was created or modified using Google's AI tools. The images are also grainy and low quality, which is typical of AI-generated content that tries to hide inconsistencies and mimic security camera footage. Additionally, the timeline doesn't add up. The picture shows Zelensky as he has appeared since the start of Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in early 2022. But Epstein died in August 2019, only a few months after Zelensky became president. There's no credible evidence that the two ever met. The Cube has trawled through the Epstein files, the latest tranche of which was released in January. And while Zelensky's name does appear in them, it's only in the context of news reports or the 2019 elections that saw him become president. This isn't the first time that false claims have tried to link Zelensky and Epstein. Other fact-checkers have debunked previous instances where pro-Russian Telegram channels spread false narratives of a connection between Zelensky, Epstein and human trafficking. Following the release of the latest files, other European politicians were falsely labelled as having links to Epstein, including France's President Emmanuel Macron and the leader of the British far-right party Reform UK, Nigel Farage.
