Warner Brothers is suing an AI image generator mid-journey, and I hope every party involved spends millions in legal fees and loses sleep. This lawsuit could set one of the most important legal precedents of the AI era, because for the first time, a major studio is directly suing an AI company for scraping its movies and TV shows to train its models. It's a textbook copyright case. Warner Brothers is saying, you're using our films to train your AI model, that's theft. And they're not wrong, but they're also Warner Brothers. The same Warner Brothers that just axed multiple finished films for tax write-offs. The same Warner Brothers that has been laying off creative staff while simultaneously pumping out AI adverts. So, don't get it twisted. They're not protecting artists, they're just protecting licensing revenue. Meanwhile, the AI companies like Midjourney and Co. have admitted that if they couldn't train their models on copyrighted art, books and movies, they literally wouldn't survive. That is their entire business model. These companies have to steal in order to stay alive. And let's not forget, Google's doing it too. They're scraping their own YouTube videos to train Gemini, they're testing automatic AI upscaling of other people's shorts, TikTok's doing the same, and Meta is doing so much worse. There are no heroes in this story. Everyone here is a tech bro or a media executive just trying to monetize the creative work of other people. At the end of the day, the toothpaste is out of the tube, the horse has left the barn, this AI is not going away. And this lawsuit, it is not justice, it's definitely not accountability.
