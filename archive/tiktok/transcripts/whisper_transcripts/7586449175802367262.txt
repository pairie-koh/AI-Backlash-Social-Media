Did you know a top motion graphics artist discovered her company was selling her creative soul to the highest bidder? Sarah Chen was the queen of animation at Sterling Creative Agency. Her work won awards, commanded premium rates, and made brands millions. Every morning, she'd craft stunning visuals that competitors could only dream of copying. Until one day, she noticed something strange in the project files. Her personal style was showing up in work credited to a mysterious junior artist named Alex Kim. But Alex Kim didn't exist. Sarah dug deeper and found the horrifying truth. For months, the agency had been feeding every frame she created into an AI training system called Jitter. They had cloned her artistic DNA without her knowledge. The next week, Sterling fired their entire motion graphics team. Sarah's 15 years of creative expertise was now being sold as a subscription service for $99 per month. Her life's work reduced to an algorithm that any competitor could rent.
