Why is it that people still talk to those who aren't there? It happens more often than most people realize. It's not always something said out loud. Sometimes it's quiet, almost automatic. Maybe a sentence spoken into a room, or a thought directed at someone who's gone. Across cultures, this isn't unusual. It's often understood in different ways. In some traditions, ancestors are believed to remain present, not physically, but as part of everyday life. People speak to them for guidance, for comfort, or simply out of habit. In other cultures, it's less about belief and more about connection. When someone has been part of daily life, their absence doesn't immediately feel real. The routines stay. The conversations almost continue. psychology describes this as a continuing bond a way the mind adapts to loss not by letting go completely but by reshaping the relationship so when people speak to someone who isn't there they're not necessarily expecting an answer they're maintaining something that mattered and that may be why this behavior appears in so many places not as a single belief but as a shared human experience.
