Lolita Gergel, the virtual singer who has become an overnight sensation in Romania. But behind her viral Balkan hits is a fierce debate over AI, identity and the line between digital creativity and cultural exploitation. Lolita isn't a person. She's an AI character created by an anonymous graphic designer known only as Tom. Her songs are inspired by old Romanian poems. Tom used generative tools to build her voice, her face and her aesthetics from scratch. Her music, a fusion of modern beats and traditional Balkan folk music, has resonated with millions. The success hasn't come without backlash. You see, activists and Roma artists are sounding the alarm, accusing the project of profiting from Roman culture. They argue that while real Roma artists are often marginalised and devalued, a virtualised digital character is now profiting from their aesthetics and spiritual references. With many saying it gives the impression that there is a platform for Romani music, but without the Romani people. Tom rejects these claims, arguing that AI has democratised creativity, allowing anyone to translate thoughts into reality without traditional barriers. Astonishingly, in a video message produced by Tom of Lolita herself defending her existence, stating that an author doesn't need to be a murderer to write a crime novel. But the controversy highlights a growing fear that AI will allow creators to bypass human experience entirely, packaging the vibes of a culture while leaving the actual community behind.
