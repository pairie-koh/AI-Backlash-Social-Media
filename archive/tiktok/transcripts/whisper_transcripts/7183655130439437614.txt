As an artist myself, who's been drawing my entire life and who plans to continue drawing for the rest of my life, I wanted to give my own current thoughts on the whole AI art controversy. It's no news to anybody that AI has been making leaps and bounds with ChatGPT, CactusAI, and of course the notorious MidJourney Art Generator. As an artist, I've heard other artists talking about it, and the main controversy lies in this idea of A, replacing artists, and B, plagiarism from the AI. I've heard a lot of artists saying that Mid Journey and other art simulators are plagiarizing from other artists and using that as a justification for why it's bad and should be canceled. Whereas that does have some merit, I feel like the crux of the issue is not that at all. Because when I think about this objectively, all the AI is doing is referencing art from all over the internet based on a prompt to generate a new image. And that's not unlike what the human brain does, right? When I wanted to make a piece of art, I looked at a bunch of images that are similar to what I'm thinking of and used those and draw elements from the different various pieces of artwork to create my own artwork. And that's how people even develop their own art styles. They look at artists they like, they take elements that they like, and put it together. So as the AI is doing essentially what the human brain does it seems a little bit off target or a little bit ridiculous to say that AI is stealing from artists but artists aren stealing from artists when they do the same thing But if you ask me I think the real concern about AI art is about phasing artists out of the artistic process On simpler terms, artists aren't going to be able to charge a lot of money for art pieces that people can just generate with AI. But I think that too is also a ridiculous claim, because at the end of the day, art is and has always been a mode of self-expression, expressing yourself through your medium. It's about communicating ideas, emotions, and themes that are so complex that they can't be said that you communicate through artwork. And obviously, AI just being a machine or a tool is completely incapable of doing that, as this is inherent to the human experience. So I think the real concern for a lot of artists, which is a valid concern, is that they can't charge, let's say, $500 for a commission anymore if somebody can just generate it with AI. But my problem with that is by looking at it in that way, all you're doing is causing the problem in which you're complaining about. What do I mean by that? The problem is if you're turning a mode of self-expression into a consumer utility, you're essentially being your own worst enemy. Because as part of the capitalistic consumerist market, the goal is to make things as simple as possible, which inevitably phases humans out and replaces them with robots. And we see this at your local CVS where self-checkout is replaced by humans. So at the end of the day, the artistic process is not going to be destroyed because of Art AI in the same way that ChatGPT isn't going to ruin writing for authors. Rather, it's going to reframe the relationship between capitalism and art.
