A part of your car is moving twice as fast as the car itself. That sounds impossible. It's not. Let's say your car is driving at 100 kilometres per hour. Look at the wheels. A rolling wheel isn't doing one motion. It's doing two at the same time. It's moving forward with the car. And it's spinning. At the very bottom of the wheel, where the tyre touches the road, something weird happens. The forward motion and the backward spin cancel out. For a split second, that point is actually not moving at all. Zero speed. Now look at the very top of the wheel. Here, the forward motion of the car and the forward motion from the spin add together. So while the car moves at 100, the top of the wheel is moving at 200 km per hour. Same wheel, same moment. Three different speeds. Zero at the bottom, car speed in the middle, double speed at the top. Every time you drive, parts of your car are breaking your intuition about motion, not breaking physics, exposing it.
