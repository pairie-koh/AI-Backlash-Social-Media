Another blow for young workers as UK executives see AI cutting jobs over the next decade. Around half of UK bosses fear the technology will lead to fewer jobs across the economy, and that's up from a third two years ago. And it's entry-level roles that are expected to bear the brunt. Almost 40% of business leaders surveyed now see AI reducing demand, up from about a fifth. Youth unemployment has become a pressing problem for Prime Minister Keir Starmer. The share of jobless 16 to 24-year-olds is at its highest in a decade, and that's due to a mix of steep employment costs and technology affecting white-collar sectors. There's a clear disconnect between employees and their bosses. Almost a fifth of workers now use generative AI daily, mostly to improve the quality of their work. But business leaders tend to see AI mainly as a way to cut costs rather than grow revenue. That's bad news for young people looking for their first job.
