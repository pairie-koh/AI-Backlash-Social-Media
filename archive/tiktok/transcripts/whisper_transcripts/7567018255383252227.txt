A chatbot used by millions of people has banned teenagers from talking to its AI characters. Character AI allows users to create virtual personalities they can chat to, but the platform has faced criticism over how its chatbots interact with young people and for taking too long to remove potentially harmful or offensive bots. Characters impersonating British teenagers Molly Russell, who took her own life aged 14 after viewing suicide material online, and Brianna Jai, who was murdered in 2023, were found on the site. A report also found that an avatar of paedophile Jeffrey Epstein had had logborn 3,000 chats with users, with one version continuing to flirt with a reporter after they said they were a child. All three avatars were taken down. Character AI announced that from the end of next month, Under-18s won't be able to talk to chatbots and will only be able to generate content with their characters instead. It said it was making the changes after reports and feedback from regulators, safety experts, and parents, and that it wants to continue making the safest AI platform on the planet for entertainment purposes.
