So here's the thing. I think the hype around AI in software development is being misunderstood or maybe I think it was just overpromised. Yes, overpromised is the right word. I actually understand where some of the AI productivity skeptics are coming from because let's be real, a lot of the marketing around AI has made it sound like you don't need software engineers anymore. AI tools and services can build entire applications on your own. You could just prompt your way to a finished product. There is no need for a team, no code basis, no engineers. And yeah, to some extent, there are tools like Replit, Lovable, and others that can generate an entire application from a single prompt. But what's getting lost in all this hype and in translation is that just because you build something doesn't mean anyone will use it. Shipping software is only one part of the equation. The hard part comes after the code runs. Will people use it? Will it scale? Can you maintain it? Can it evolve over time? If you've been in the industry long enough, then you know this. Writing the code is just the beginning. If no one uses your application, it's no different from a side project that sits abandoned in a GitHub repository. At the end of the day, it's still just an idea. It's a starting point. So when the critics are saying we should be seeing more products out in the world if AI is really making development easier, that logic doesn't quite hold because the bottleneck isn't just code. It's execution, it's distribution, it's product market fit, and everything that comes after that. AI can help you build faster, but it doesn't guarantee that people will care. So yeah, the hype doesn't quite match reality, but it never does. That's why we call it hype. It doesn't mean AI hasn't improved the development experience, it just means we need to reset the narrative. AI isn't replacing developers. Instead, it's reducing friction, helping us unblock faster, and helping us move from idea to prototype with less pain.
