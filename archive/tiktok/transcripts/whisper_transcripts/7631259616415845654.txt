If you think that 62 million visits to a website that contained advice on how to drug and assault your partner is unrealistic or inflated, consider this number. 483 million. That's the total number of deepfake app downloads, according to a recent tech transparency project investigation. And this refers specifically to apps that are used to create sexually explicit, usually non-consensual content. As Laura Bates points out in her recent book, the new age of misogyny, these apps don't even properly work on the images and videos of men. They were designed and are used to objectify, to harass, to abuse and degrade women and girls. Some social media apps, like the one formerly known as Twitter, allow people to create such content as well. And according to another recent study done by the Center for Countering Digital Hate, in just 11 days in January, it likely produced around 3 million of such images. thousands of which were of children. So who's generating all of this? All of this disgusting advice and disturbing content? Who is consuming it? Is it really just a few men, a few bad apples? You don't get to 483 million downloads of deep fake apps that are then used to objectify, abuse and degrade women if it's just a fringe minority of men doing it.
