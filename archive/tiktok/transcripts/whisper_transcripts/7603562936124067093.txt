Bunnings has won the right to use facial recognition technology across its stores in a decision that could be just the beginning for other Australian retailers. Bunnings had deployed facial recognition in 62 stores before it was shut down by the Privacy Commissioner last year. The technology scans the faces of people entering their stores, checking it against images of people who have been banned. If there's no match, The images are then deleted. But that raised concerns about privacy, surrounding the retention, albeit for only a fraction of a second, of sensitive data. Bunnings appealed, arguing it needs the scans to prevent violent attacks against its staff and customers, even releasing CCTV of incidents with guns and knives alongside a nude man wandering the aisle in Tari. The hardware giant also argues the technology reduces theft, claiming at least 66% of losses can be attributed to the top 10% of offenders. And that's been upheld with the Administrative Tribunal saying it can be used for the limited purpose of combating very significant retail crime and protecting their staff and customers from violence, abuse and intimidation within its stores. It could now open up the same justification for other retailers to do the same in their stores.
