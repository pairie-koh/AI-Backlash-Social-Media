MIT researchers ran the first brain scan study of people using ChatGPT to write essays, and the results were surprising. They followed 54 people for four months. One group used ChatGPT, one group used search engines, and one group wrote the essays entirely on their own. Researchers monitored their brains using EEG scans, and the differences were striking. People writing without AI showed the strongest brain connectivity, multiple regions of the brain actively working together. ChatGPT users showed the weakest neural engagement, but the most surprising finding came afterwards. 83% of the ChatGPT users couldn't quote a single sentence from essays they had written just moments earlier. They had almost no memory of what they produced. They then asked the ChatGPT group to write without AI, and their brain stayed under-engaged. Researchers called this effect cognitive debt. In other words, when a tool does the thinking for you, your brain practices thinking less. So the lesson isn't don't use AI. It's how you use it. Use it to challenge your thinking, not replace it. Because the goal is in faster writing, it's a stronger mind.
