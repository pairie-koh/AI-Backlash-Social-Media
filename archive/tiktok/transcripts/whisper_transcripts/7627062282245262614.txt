In a stunning report from The Guardian, experts warn that Australia's rush to automate environmental assessments is leading to catastrophic errors. The goal sounded perfect. Use AI to speed up planning approvals for mines, dams and housing developments. But the investigation found these black box systems are consistently failing to detect endangered species and critical ecosystems. In some cases, the AI tools erased entire protected wetlands from digital maps, giving developers the green light to destroy habitats that should have been off limits. These environmental tools are operating with zero human oversight. See, decision makers are deferring to authoritative algorithms, assuming it's more accurate than a biologist in the field. But the AI is only as good as its data. And right now it's suffering from massive hallucinations. claiming land is barren when it's actually teething with life. And these are high stakes. Once an old forest is cleared based on flawed AI report, you can't undo the damage. Legal experts warn that we are creating an accountability vacuum. If an AI mistakenly approves a project that causes local extinction, who's responsible? The software company, the government, or the developer who followed the machine's lead? As we integrate AI into environmental law, we need human eyes on the ground. We can't let efficiency become the excuse for an ecological disaster.
