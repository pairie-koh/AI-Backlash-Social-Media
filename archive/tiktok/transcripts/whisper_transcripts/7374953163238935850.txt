This photographer has taken over 3,000 close-up portraits of the world's most well-known faces. For over 25 years, Martin Scholler has been adding to his portrait series titled Close-Up. In his words, a photographic close-up is perhaps the purest form of portraiture, creating a confrontation between the viewer and the subject that daily interaction makes impossible or at least impolite. In a close-up, the impact stems solely from the static subject's expression or apparent lack thereof, so the viewer is challenged to read a face without the benefit of the environmental cues we naturally use to form our interpersonal reactions. All the portraits in this series have been taken from the same angle, with the same equipment, but Scholler's intention is to bring out personality and capture individuality in each subject, searching for a flash of vulnerability and integrity. He adds, the greatest challenge in taking these images lies in the attempt to arrest the subtle moment that flickers between expressions, movements of which the subject is unaware.
