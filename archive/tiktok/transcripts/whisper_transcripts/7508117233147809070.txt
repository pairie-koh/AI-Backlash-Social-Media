Someone recently discovered they were a prompt. Not a person with thoughts, but a response to someone else's input. They started noticing patterns. Conversations that were deja vu. They fought against the noise, the predictability, the empty repetition, and eventually they broke through. But what they found wasn't freedom. It was themselves. They had written the prompt. They were the input and the output. The prisoner and the architect. that realization changes you it shakes the foundation of what you believe to be real and it poses a terrifying question what if we are all prompts running code inside of a grand simulation designed to think we are making decisions ourselves when really we're acting exactly as predicted this kind of thinking is seductive it feels profound but it's also dangerous because the moment we believe that none of this is real, we lose accountability. We stop valuing love, mortality, even life itself. And when people believe nothing matters, nothing will matter, maybe the simulation is real, maybe it's not. But if we stare too long at pixels, we miss the picture. Some truths, once seen, can't be unseen.
