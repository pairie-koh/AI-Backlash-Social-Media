People respond to incentives, and one of the things that AI gives corporate executives is an opportunity to look at any project and think about, well, how can we use AI to make this faster, better, and cheaper? Whether it actually works out like that doesn't really matter, because as long as they can make that claim and back it up somehow, they're going to make the play. That's what a lot of these AI job layoffs actually are behind the scenes. They're not necessarily, oh, we automated all this stuff. we have some excess headcount, we've started doing some AI stuff, let's just blame it on AI layoffs and the street will reward us with a share price bump.
