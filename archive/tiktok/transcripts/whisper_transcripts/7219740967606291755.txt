Did scientists in Japan create artificial intelligence that can read your mind? Scientists at Osaka University used an AI model called stable diffusion, which analyzed the MRI scans of people who were presented with about 10,000 different images. And when they compared the AI results to the images presented to the test subjects, they were pretty close! While this isn't full-blown mind reading in the sense of sound and motion and sensations, it's still a major breakthrough in cognitive pattern recognition that AI could soon learn to identify. Scientists do acknowledge the problems that could arise from this technology. For one, government oversight. Which, yeah, walking down the street and monitors being put in place to read your mind is not great. Other issues include your thoughts being used for data collection, marketing, or even legal cases. Where your thoughts could literally be used against you. However, AI is only as good as the data it's given. We don't have a full framework for how the brain processes the visual world in the first place. The brain scanning technology itself has its own limitations. So we can't yet provide AI with everything it would need to translate dreams, for example. In addition to that, the model in this study was based on the test subjects and how they process visual information. So this technology wouldn't necessarily be applicable to everyone. Yet.
