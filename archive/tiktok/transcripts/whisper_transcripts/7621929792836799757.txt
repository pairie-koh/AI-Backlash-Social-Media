There's a lot of misinformation circulating about the Iran war. Supposed missile attacks, AI-generated videos of destruction, and even a death hoax. It can be difficult to tell what's real from fake. So here are three tips for verifying posts you see online. First, be on the lookout for recycled footage. You can use reverse image search tools such as Google Images and Tindai. Check to see if these images or videos were posted online before the war began in February 2026. For example, this post claims that the Lebanese army in Hezbollah have captured multiple towns in northern Israel. But a reverse image search showed that photo was taken during a 2023 military exercise. Second, if you think a video might be AI-generated, examine it for inconsistencies. Many times, there are obvious giveaways. This can be illegible text, street, or business signs that don't match any real places. Sometimes, it's as obvious as a watermark. If you look closer at these images supposedly showing US soldiers captured by Iran, you'll see the Gemini logo, which is Google's AI tool, on the bottom right. Some video generators can make clips only up to 8 seconds long, so if a video is around that length or is composed of multiple 8-second cuts, there's a chance AI was involved. The audio can also be a clue. Is the background too quiet? Are the voices understandable? If there are no obvious signs of AI, look for news reports or other documentation that proves the event really happened. If it's a high-profile event, realistically, there should be credible reports about it. Third, pick your sources wisely. Many accounts that share misinformation have blue check marks signaling they can be trusted and claim to be news sources, but they're not affiliated with any credible organizations. Be wary of what AI chatbots say. They have no ability to discern accuracy and can, and do, get things wrong. Follow sources you trust and use them to stay informed.
