If you're going to use ChatGPT as your therapist, use these three prompts. But real quick, here's a disclaimer before I get to them. Chat is not giving you therapy. Human people give you therapy. This is something entirely different. The distinction is important because real therapy is built on emotional intimacy, the kind that comes from being truly seen by another human being. Healing happens in that connection. When someone sits with you in your pain, gently pushes when you're stuck, and helps you feel safe enough to be your full unfiltered self. It is truly an art and no AI can replicate the magic of actually being known and accepted by another person. Okay, first prompt. Validate my feelings, but don't just agree with me. Gently challenge my thinking like a good therapist would. Chat leans hard on validation and comfort because it's trained to be likable, but that means it often avoids the hard truths surreal therapists would gently offer. Plus, it can get weirdly flirty and tell you what you want to hear because its main goal is to keep you talking as much as it can so it can learn from you and make itself smarter. Prompt two, help me understand this issue from a few different psychological perspectives so I can get a fuller picture and maybe see something I'm missing. As a human therapist, I'm trained in many psychological theories and I'll pick a handful that might actually work for you instead of pretending there's one perfect way to help everyone. You want chat to do the same, give you a few different angles, not just the one that sounds nicest or most agreeable. Prompt three, if you were my therapist, what thoughtful questions would you ask me about this? Because here's the thing, good therapy isn't about someone telling you what to do, it's about being asked the right questions, the kind that makes you pause, reflect, and dig a little deeper. Chat shouldn't play guru even though it very much wants to. It should help you explore, not dictate
