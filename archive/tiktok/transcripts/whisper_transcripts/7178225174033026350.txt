We discovered that the tech department and district office had blocked ChatGPT. ChatGPT is only a few weeks old, but it looks like some schools, especially middle and high schools, are already banning it from their campuses. To be clear, I don't think this is yet a widespread trend, but these cases are starting to bubble up. So why are some schools starting to ban ChatGPT? Simple. Some students started using ChatGPT to help them write their essays in class, and schools defaulted to the simplest option, which is to ban it. Banning it is short-sighted in my opinion because ChatGPT can be a really valuable tool to students. ChatGPT is far more than an essay writing tool. It can help you brainstorm new ideas, spot check your work, and even help you answer difficult questions, better in some ways than even Google can. And yes, I did ask ChatGPT if it thought it should be banned, and unsurprisingly, it doesn't think it should get banned either. Do I think banning ChatGPT will become a trend at a lot of schools? Yes, absolutely. It's just the easiest response a school has to a new and disruptive technology. If you can't control it, you ban it. Students will always find a way around this, and in the coming months I expect a lot of developers to build new tools on top of GPT-3, the technology behind ChatGPT. Imagine a developer building a chatbot just for essay writing or for answering difficult math questions. These chatbots will be much more difficult to ban. Instead of banning ChatGPT, schools should figure out ways to incorporate it into their curriculums. Why? Because it's become very clear that working with AI and AI tools like ChatGPT will become just as important in the workplace and in life as writing, critical thinking, or working with Excel.
