Did you know that one of World War II's greatest art thefts was carried out with dirty laundry? In 1945, U.S. Army Lieutenant Joe Tom Meador walked into Germany's Quedlinburg Cathedral. Inside were priceless medieval treasures, thousand-year-old manuscripts, golden reliquaries, artifacts worth millions. Meador had a simple plan. He carefully wrapped each treasure and stuffed them into his laundry bags. Then he shipped everything home to Texas like dirty clothes. For decades, the stolen relics sat hidden in his family's house, the perfect crime. But in the 1990s, Mieder died. His heirs found the treasures and tried to sell them. That's when everything unraveled. Art experts immediately recognized the missing Quedlinburg artifacts. An international legal battle erupted. German officials demanded their return. The family fought back, claiming they legally owned the stolen goods. After years in court, most treasures were returned to Germany for $12 million. Midor's brilliant wartime heist was finally exposed, all because his family got greedy.
