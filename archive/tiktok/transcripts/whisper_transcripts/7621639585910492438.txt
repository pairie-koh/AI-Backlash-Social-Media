He stole $8 million from thousands of artists and nobody even noticed. Starting in 2017, Michael Smith used AI to generate hundreds of thousands of songs, then built an army of thousands of bot accounts to stream them billions of times across Spotify and other audio providers. But what makes this plan genius is also what makes it a crime. Streaming royalties come from a shared pool of money, so every fake stream he raked in was a stolen directly from a real artist. To dodge detection, he spread the streams across thousands of accounts and thousands of different tracks, so no single song looked suspicious. By the time the mechanical licensing collective caught on, he'd pocketed over $8 million. Smith pleaded guilty to wire fraud conspiracy in 2024 and will forfeit every cent, but with a maximum sentence of only five years. But this case only raises an important question. If one person with a laptop could steal $8 million from artists, how many others are doing it right now?
