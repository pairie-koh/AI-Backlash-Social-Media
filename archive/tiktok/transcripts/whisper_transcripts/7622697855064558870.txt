Platinum is so absurdly rare, if you melted down all the platinum ever mined, it would barely fill a small living room. A small living room, not a warehouse. And yet this quiet silver metal is one of the strangest things on earth. It is denser than lead. A single cubic metre weighs about 21.5 tonnes. Imagine a bathtub-sized block of it, and your floor is gone. It laughs at corrosion. Air barely touches it. Acid struggles with it. Time for platinum is more of a suggestion. Then it gets weird. This metal helps clean the poison out of car exhausts, speeds up brutal chemical reactions, and sits inside equipment built to survive ridiculous heat because platinum doesn't melt until around 1,768 degrees Celsius. That is hotter than lava. Hot enough to turn ordinary metals into regret. and despite being jewellery famous, platinum's real superpower isn't beauty, it's usefulness. It works while refusing to react, which sounds impossible, like a firefighter made of gasoline. Platinum isn't just precious, it is industrial sorcery in metallic form.
