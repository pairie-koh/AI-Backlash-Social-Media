Everyone's hyping up OpenClaw right now. I've been using it for weeks and yeah, it's cool. But I've been running into the same problems. The setup is messy, my keys are stored in plain text, and it literally does something stupid and I can't even stop it. And that's when I decided to go test that PokeClaw side by side. It's basically the same concept. You give it a task, it works across your apps and gets the job done. And it literally connects to over a thousand apps with Gmail, Jira, Slack, GitHub, Notion. Basically anything you already use. But here's where it gets interesting. I gave both OpenClaw and PokeClaw the same task. I both had to pull data from my Notion, cross-reference the data on Google Sheets, then draft a summary report on my Google Doc, and then email it to my team using my Gmail. Literally four different apps and one prompt. And the results were insane. Open Claw, it kept losing context between steps, burned through a whole bunch of tokens, and I literally had to reprompt halfway through. Whereas PokeClaw did everything with 70% less token usage. It pulled the data, it matched it with my Google Sheets, wrote the entire report on my Google Doc, prepared the email, and all it did was wait for my approval to send. And the next day when I asked it to update the report, it remembered everything including the format, the structure, so I didn't have to re-explain anything to Pokeclaw. And the best part was the way it was set up. You don't need a Mac Mini, there's no local setups required, or any terminal commands. All you have to do is log in and you can just get started. And those who are concerned about the security, it's not even closed. All my credentials were encrypted, agents are sandboxed, and there's always an approval required whenever something sensitive needs to be sent out. Openclaw could never do this. And I'm not saying that Openclaw is bad, but if you're doing real work with it, this is the difference that actually matters.
