Warner Bros. has joined the fight against AI copyright infringement, and it's got some pretty damning evidence in its favor. Welcome to Stage Runner, your source for what's shooting and where. In case you missed it, Hollywood is finally fighting back against AI image and video generators that steal and replicate copyrighted material. In June, Disney and Universal filed a lawsuit against AI image generator Midjourney on the grounds of copyright infringement. The studios say Midjourney is a bottomless pit of plagiarism and quote, piracy is piracy. They hope to stop Midjourney and other for-profit image generators from using and recreating their content, and now Warner Bros. has filed its own lawsuit against Midjourney, saying the AI company reasonably dispenses WBD's intellectual property. You see, Midjourney does charge users a fee to subscribe, and the lawsuit posits that Midjourney's ability to recreate copyrighted characters is a clear draw for subscribers. And Warner Bros. has proof. The lawsuit includes some obvious examples of Midjourney recreating Warner Bros. copyrighted materials. According to The Hollywood Reporter, Warner Bros. is seeking Mid Journey's profits attributable to the alleged infringement, or alternatively, $150,000 per infringed work.
