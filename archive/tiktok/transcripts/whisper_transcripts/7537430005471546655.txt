Sonnet number three by William Shakespeare. Look in thy glass, and tell the face thou viewest, Now is the time that face should form another. Whose fresh repair, if now, thou not renewest, Thou dost beguile the world, unbless some mother. For where is she so fair Whose uneared wound disdains the tillage of thy husbandry? Or who is he so fond will be the tomb Of his self-love to stop posterity? Thou art thy mother's glass, And she in thee calls back the lovely April of her prime. So thou through windows of thine age shalt see Despite of wrinkles, this is thy golden time But if thou live remembered not to be Die single, and thine image dies with thee
