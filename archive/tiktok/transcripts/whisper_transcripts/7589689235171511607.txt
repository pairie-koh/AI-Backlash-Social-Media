Whenever I talk to people about AI regulation, they say, well, we don't want to overregulate because then we'll fall behind China. What's your perspective on that? My view is that if you ask the American people, which polling has done over and over again, we get a very consistent message, which is in the United States, people are still more fearful than excited about AI. They are excited. They see the possibilities, but they are still more fearful. And people are smart. They saw how jobs went overseas when we didn't deal with the changes as trade globalized as automation came to manufacturing. And they have seen what social media has done. Yes, it connected to us, to our family and friends. But yes, it also created this horrible polarization. It's created mental health issues for our kids. And people know that if we just do more of that with AI, that that's not the future that we want to live in. And so to me, getting the guardrails in place and doing the policy work that goes along with this push from the market, that's how we can get to a good place. But right now, we haven't been able to get that done. And I think it really, to me, it reflects more the lack of ability in our hyper-partisan environment that we're in, our lack of ability to come together and do the things that many people on both sides of the aisle know need to get done.
