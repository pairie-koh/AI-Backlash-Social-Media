As cities adopt more renewable energy, keeping the electrical grid stable becomes more complicated. Solar, wind, and battery sources behave differently from traditional systems, and utilities need better forecasting tools. This is where Dr. Jermall Jones, an energy systems engineer, focuses his research. Jones develops machine learning models that predict how renewable energy flows through local grids throughout the day. His models track sunlight patterns, wind speed, temperature changes, and energy usage trends to forecast when grids will need support or redistribution. These predictions help utilities balance the flow of energy so that neighborhoods receive consistent power even when renewable sources fluctuate. Jones also studies how to integrate community-level energy storage so that excess power generated during strong sunlight can support homes during evening hours. His work is part of a growing effort to modernize energy systems in a way that is sustainable, reliable, and accessible to communities with older infrastructure. Every time a renewable-powered grid runs smoothly through shifting conditions, the principles behind Jones's modeling work are part of the solution.
