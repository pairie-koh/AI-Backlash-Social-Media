In the age of AI, has my stance on free art aged like milk? No, quite the opposite. Yes, AI companies violated copyright law to train their art generators. And they're getting away with it because copyright law was never about protecting artists. And fixing AI to work within copyright law is the worst possible outcome. Now, let's first rewind a little. In 2001 I realized art is speech and that like speech it must be free and for it to be free we need to abolish copyright. This isn't about selling art but about the rights to reproduce the art once it's sold or released. Art must be in the public domain. Around the 2010s I started realizing that copyright abolition needs to go hand in hand with greater protection of personal information. Social media companies like Facebook rose to monopoly status by two means. Owning the exclusive rights to the code on which the platform is built and tracking our personal information. With those two they locked us into their platforms and earned money selling our info to advertisers and companies like Cambridge Analytica Both of these practices need to be illegal Now the AI companies are trying to become monopolies of their own and are entering into licensing agreements with the likes of Disney and the major record companies. This will end in monopolies not only owning the platforms, our personal information and our shared cultural and artistic inheritance, but also owning the tools to create competitive work. If Disney and Microsoft and Spotify manage to create their dream of viable and good AI art generation tools, that means the death of creativity, the death of independent art, the death of an alternative, because everything will go through the Disney filter and feed the monopoly. There is no viable solution to all of this without the abolition of all copyright and exclusive use of intellectual property so that all art is free and all software open source. In addition, we need strict laws protecting the spread of personal information. So abolish copyright and protect people. Make free art or die.
