AI-enabled cameras monitoring polling areas in real time, detecting crowd build-ups or suspicious movements. Police equipped with body-worn cameras recording everyone near polling stations. This is the plan for the West Bengal elections. It's projected to be the most extensive security deployment in India's electoral history. The Election Commission of India has even set up a central AI control room. It will monitor tens of thousands of polling stations. Authorities say it is necessary to ensure security after so many cases of election violence. But multiple cameras even set up in voting booths also raise questions about surveillance. Even more, an AI algorithm used to clean up voting lists has sparked outrage. The ECI used AI to flag discrepancies between voter lists and their documents. Authorities said they wanted to erase names of duplicate and dead voters. But critics say millions of people were wrongly removed due to spelling differences in names or unrealistic criteria the AI used. They also say specific communities like Muslims, Matawas, and Rajbangshis were targeted. Still, despite the dispute, the ECI continued to use the software. Do you think AI can make elections safer?
