How are fake videos of frontline Ukrainian soldiers fooling online users? Videos of Ukrainian soldiers on the frontline have become a staple of modern-day warfare, more than four years into Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Here you can see a Ukrainian soldier filming trenches lined with bodies of deceased and injured soldiers. These videos shared on social media catalogued the chilling reality of many Ukrainians battling off Russian soldiers. troops from the front line. This kind of footage has become an essential tool in wartime messaging, but in this case you may have been fooled. This video, like many others, is in fact synthetic. It forms part of a whole series of videos of fake Ukrainian soldiers that were uncovered and analyzed by experts at Sensity, a forensic AI detection company. These kinds of videos are typically initially shared on platforms such as TikTok and Telegram on accounts that are later removed. Once a video is posted and gains traction through likes, views and comments, it's then shared on adjacent platforms such as X, Facebook, Instagram or YouTube. The content is then picked up by established Russian influence networks, including military bloggers or Russian state-owned propaganda sites. Sensity researchers also found recurring patterns in the rhetoric pushed by AI soldiers, notably in an effort to demoralize real troops. For example, Therefore, they often spread claims of corruption in the Ukrainian army and encourage surrender. Some videos, such as this one in which a serviceman claims he no longer wants to serve in the Ukrainian army due to poor leadership, aim to damage trust in Ukraine's army command. They also try to shift the blame from Russia to Ukrainian leadership and make Ukraine's defeat seem inevitable by showing exhausted units willing to surrender.
