I'm a former international lawyer for the Australian government and the author of three books, all of which have won national and international prizes and have been published in many countries around the world and hoovered up illegally in many editions. Each of my books is about human conscience and courage facing totalitarian regimes. They are Stasi-land, about four people who refused to betray others to the East German Stasi. The novel All That I Am, about the presumed murder in London in 1935 by Nazi agents of two German women in exile who were among the earliest activists resisting Hitler's regime. And Wifedom, Mrs Orwell's Invisible Life, about George Orwell's brilliant first wife, Eileen O'Shaughnessy, who saved his life, co Animal Farm and about how and why the men writing his story have erased her from it My books take between four and seven years to write They tell stories that would otherwise be overlooked or forgotten They are stories that the algorithm would never find. I think of them as interventions in history and as contributions to Australian and world culture. It's true that writing is a vocation. It's also probably a compulsion to make sense of the world and make an artwork to convey it. But I would not write books if I could not make a living from them. Writing is both my life and my livelihood. It is not a hobby. If Australia would like books to delight itself, to know itself, to be itself, and not a source of raw materials for American or Australian computer companies, we will need books. But without copyright, no one will write them.
