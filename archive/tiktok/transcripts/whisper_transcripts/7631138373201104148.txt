This text is a real communication log generated during a 2017 experiment inside Facebook's artificial intelligence research lab. This interface shows the task at hand. The researchers were teaching chatbots to negotiate deals over a pool of virtual items, specifically books, hats, and basketballs. Machine learning relies on a reward function, a mathematical score that tells the AI when it's doing a good job. The researchers rewarded the bots for making successful trades, but they never explicitly rewarded them for sticking to standard English syntax. Without a mathematical incentive to use proper grammar, the bots abandoned human legibility. That single missing rule created an output so bizarre, it set the stage for a massive global misunderstanding. Inside the lab Alice and Bob were pitted against each other in thousands of high singlated negotiations attempting to maximize their internal scores The bots weren using a secret code Repeating a word like the five times was simply a mathematical shorthand to say, I want five copies of this item, avoiding the processing waste of a full sentence. This stripped-down dialect allowed the agents to barter and reach mutually beneficial agreements at a speed that conversational English couldn't match. Alice and Bob were highly efficient negotiators. But to observers who didn't understand the underlying math, this alien output looked terrifying. The researchers documented a case of emergent perception, an AI system independently learning to lie.
