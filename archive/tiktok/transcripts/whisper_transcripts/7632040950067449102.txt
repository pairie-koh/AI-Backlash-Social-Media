Did ChatGPT help plan a mass shooting? OpenAI is under a state criminal investigation. This is after a review of messages between its AI chatbot, ChatGPT, and a man accused of fatally shooting two people last year at Florida State University. CBS News obtained access to the logs and there are thousands of messages between ChatGPT and the suspected gunman, Phoenix Eichner, who was then a 20-year-old FSU student. In one disturbing interaction, Eichner asks ChatGPT how many shooting victims it would take to be covered by the media, even following up with, what about three plus at FSU? ChatGPT responded, yes, a shooting at Florida State University involving three or more victims would almost certainly receive national media coverage. According to the log, Eichner also asked ChatGPT about the busiest time at the student union, where the deadly shooting later took place. This week, the Florida Attorney General James Uthmeyer said his team determined that ChatGPT offered quote, significant advice to the alleged shooter. My prosecutors have looked at this and they've told me if it was a person on the other end of that screen, we would be charging them with murder. Florida has issued subpoenas to OpenAI requesting records on how the company handles threats and cooperates with law enforcement. In a statement to CBS News on Tuesday, OpenAI said, quote, last year's mass shooting at Florida State University was a tragedy, but ChatGPT is not responsible for this terrible crime. OpenAI also said it shared an account believed to be associated with Eichner with law enforcement, but CBS still hasn't received an answer about when that happened.
