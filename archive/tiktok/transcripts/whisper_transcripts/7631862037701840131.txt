AI is everywhere in acquisition right now. Creatives, data, campaign management. But here's the thing, using AI doesn't mean better performance. Sometimes it actually makes things worse. The first thing to accept, AI is no longer what sets you apart. It's the baseline. Everyone is using it to move faster, test more, and produce at scale. So the real question isn't should you use AI, it's are you using it the right way? And here's where most teams go wrong. They rely on AI to analyze performance and make decisions. Why did this drop? Should I scale this? AI will give you an answer, fast, and it will sound convincing. But it doesn't know your constraints, your history, or your product. It's working with an incomplete picture, and one missing piece of context can send you in the wrong direction. There's another layer to this. You're not the only one optimizing. Platforms are already doing it. Meta, Google, TikTok, they optimize for their own metrics, not yours. So yeah, a creative might get pushed hard because it drives click, even if they bring no value to you. Without a real oversight, you're not following a strategy anymore. You're just following the algorithm. And on the creative side, same story. AI helps you produce much more. But when everyone uses the same tool, everything starts to look the same. Same hooks, same concepts, nothing that really stands out. What makes the difference today is not AI, it's how you use it. The teams that win aren't the ones using the most AI. They're the ones who never stop thinking for themselves.
