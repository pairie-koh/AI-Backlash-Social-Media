A few weeks ago, a designer at Pinterest went viral for sharing that she was part of their latest 15% workforce reduction. The catch, however, was that when their CEO shared news of the layoffs, he said it was because they were doubling down on an AI future. The only problem, that designer was already working on AI features. In fact, she had just shipped an AI feature a few months prior. And Pinterest isn't the only company doing this. Like in 2025, AI was blamed for 50,000 layoffs. But when Forrester researchers dug a little deeper, they found that the majority of those companies don't actually have mature AI applications that can take over this work. They're calling it AI washing, aka blaming future robots for today's budget cuts. And this is why we don't only read the headlines. Forrester is estimating that only 6% of jobs will actually be automated by 2030. And more than half of those companies will end up walking back those layoffs because they realize they moved way too fast. Amazon, for example, has already reversed their CEO's claims that the 16,000 layoffs they did last June were part of this AI-empowered workforce optimization. Now they're just calling it reducing bureaucracy. So why is everybody obsessed with AI? Well, maybe it's because saying something like we're investing in modern, efficient workforce optimization innovation technology sounds a lot better than just saying we're having money problems. Or maybe it's because it's a lot safer than blaming the economic policies that come out of a governing body that gets its feelings hurt every time someone disagrees with them.
