Worried about losing your job to artificial intelligence? Economist Carmelo Frilito has a different take. In a letter to the editor, he explains that every major technological breakthrough invariably brings with it a familiar wave of anxiety. From the Luddites' smashing looms in the 1800s to fears over electricity and computers, history shows the same pattern. Jobs change, but they don't vanish. Instead, productivity rises, costs fall, and human wants expand. When machines free up labor, workers shift into new industries—manufacturing, services, healthcare, education, technology—fields earlier generations couldn't even imagine. Artificial intelligence is no different, he assures. Yes, it automates routine tasks, But it also creates opportunities, complements human skills, and reshapes work rather than erasing it. Just as spreadsheets didn't eliminate accountants, AI is more likely to empower workers than replace them, he says. The Austrian School of Economics reminds us that the economy isn't static. It is driven by creativity, entrepreneurship, and evolving human wants. Lower costs mean greater purchasing power, which sparks demand for new goods, services, and jobs. Transitions can be disruptive, but they're not the end of work. AI doesn't act alone. It's shaped by entrepreneurs and consumers.
