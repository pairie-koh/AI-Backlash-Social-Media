Something altogether puzzling is happening in Australia's universities since our expose on the rampant rate of AI cheating by students. We'll use AI to cheat the test and then they'll use AI to mark the tests. We have obtained a report that shows Australia's universities have all but abandoned AI detection on their campuses. Why? Because most institutions have no faith in detection software to accurately flag misconduct amongst students, partly because students are now so good at cheating. Because it's just so easy to fabricate your own work and fabricate any kind of assignment or quiz, because it's just too easy. Which leaves us with the curious situation where many universities, worried about student blowback and reputational damage from false accusations, are now teaching their students how to defend themselves if ever they're accused of AI cheating. How has academia got to this? If they can change this stereotype that AI is cheating and change it to AI is a learning tool, then that is the best way to go about it. Chinese international student Albert thinks he knows. In an exclusive interview, he tells me that 100% of his international cohort at Adelaide University is using AI to pass their exams and complete coursework wherever possible. What's more, he argues, AI is a better teacher than his human tutors. And when you're paying $100,000 for your master's degree, Albert says it's pretty obvious his university cares more about his money than his education.
