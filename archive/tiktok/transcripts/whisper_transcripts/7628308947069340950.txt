The CEO of the world's most evil tech company says AI will destroy humanities jobs. The CEO of Palantir, Alex Karp, has come out and said the quiet part out loud, that AI is going to destroy jobs, and in the same breath he reassures everyone that there will still be more than enough jobs, just not for the people who studied in humanities and arts. He specifically said that it will affect democratic voters and women, but will not affect the economic power of the working class and male voters. According to him, the future belongs to people with vocational training, or those with very specific standout cognitive traits. Hands-on physical or highly specialized roles like electricians, engineers, and technicians are much harder to automate. That part does track. The labor market has always shifted with technology, and this is just another iteration of that. But the framing is diabolical, because it assumes that humanities are just expendable. It assumes that critical thinking, ethics, history, philosophy, the ability to interpret society, question power, understand human behavior, are somehow less valuable because they don't produce immediate measurable output. And that is a dangerously narrow way of looking at the world. Especially when the very technologies causing the disruption are raising massive ethical, political and societal questions that require exactly those skills to navigate. After all, who benefits from a world where people are told that their value comes down purely to physical output? Humanities don't just teach content, they teach people how to question, critique and push back. A future that sidelines those skills is a terrifying one to imagine.
