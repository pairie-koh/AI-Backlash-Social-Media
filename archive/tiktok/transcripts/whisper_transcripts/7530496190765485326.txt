Trump's highly anticipated AI action plan just dropped a few hours ago. It's 28 pages long and has 90 plus policy proposals. The plan itself is largely built around three main pillars. The first one being accelerating AI innovation, the second building domestic AI infrastructure, and the third leading in international diplomacy and security. The first pillar means that they naturally want US dominance of the most powerful AI systems. And the strategy here isn't that the government is going to build more AI, but rather that the government is moving out of the way for Silicon Valley. This means things like removing onerous regulation and red tape seen as hindering progress, encouraging more open source and open weight models because closed AI systems are kind of seen as stifling competition and innovation. They're going to heavily push AI adoption in sectors like manufacturing, science, and defense. And they're also mandating that any AI used by the federal government must be free of top-down ideological bias. This essentially takes aim at what it calls woke AI. In this sense, the government isn't just buying models that work, They're also buying models that agree with their political philosophy, which means that AI systems might literally reflect different political regimes. On the flip side, removing a lot of regulatory friction could supercharge innovation, but potentially at the cost of proper guardrails. It basically a classic move fast break things moment and the bet is that speed wins the race Pillar 2 is all about building and building big with this they going to fast track permits for data centers chip fabs and any energy infrastructure they also rolling back environmental and dei regulations and also offering federal support for mega infrastructure projects like stargate which is a 500 billion dollar plan with openai oracle and softbank to build compute capacity in the us this pillar essentially shows that this administration thinks that physical infrastructure is the new competitive advantage. If you think about chips and compute as being the new oil, then the U.S. wants to own the refineries and the pipeline. But also the unfortunate trade-off is that we'll likely see less environmental oversight, leading potentially to more gas-powered energy plants and higher carbon emissions. And then pillar three is all about how AI supremacy is a national security imperative. This means that they want to block China's influence and reshape global standards to align with the US, meaning more US-made models, chips, and software for allies, and helping US companies expand AI abroad. The big goal here is to make American AI the default in schools, in the military, in other governments. It's essentially a strategy straight out of the Cold War playbook to align countries through tech exports. What's pretty clear here in the action plan is that Silicon Valley is more influential than ever in national policy, and that the safety and environmental conversation was very much so left on the sidelines. The big question here is, are we building wisely or just building fast?
