Does AI really use so much water that it's drying up towns? That's what the headlines want you to believe. Legacy media keeps warning that AI data centers are guzzling ungodly amounts of water, even claiming people's taps are running dry. But when you actually look at the data, the story falls apart. Substack writer Andy Masley did exactly that. In 2023, US data centers used about 200 to 250 million gallons of water per day. That sounds huge until you zoom out. America uses 132 billion gallons of water every single day. That means data centers account for just 0.2% of total water use. In that town where the New York Times said taps ran dry, it wasn't because AI used up the water. It was caused by sediment getting into groundwater during construction. A real issue, but once construction ended, the problem was fixed. Even if data centers' power and water use quadruple by 2030, they'd still use only 8% of the water used by golf courses and 1% of the water used for corn farming. And remember, water is a renewable resource. Using water is not the same thing as destroying it. To be clear, this level of water use is not nothing. The AI is also an economic engine. Data centers bring hundreds of millions of dollars in tax revenue and often fund major upgrades to local water treatment systems. Yet residents are often told only the fear story. In Indianapolis, activists celebrated after stopping a Google data center that would have brought the city $1 billion in investment, saying it would have used 1 million gallons of water per day. That's just 0.4% of the county's daily water use. There are many things to be concerned about when it comes to AI and its impact on our culture and workforce, but water usage should not be one of them.
