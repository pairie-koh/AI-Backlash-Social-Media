A solo researcher at Google just dropped the paper claiming that AI could never, not in 10 years or 100 years, actually be conscious. And the logic behind it is kinda hard to argue with. Right now you have serious scientists debating things like AI rights and legal protections for AI models. Their whole argument is built on this idea that if you make language models complex and big enough and throw crazy compute at it with giant data centers, consciousness will just show up. And in the paper he gives an example. A map of New York City is not New York City. No matter how detailed the map gets, doesn't matter how many streets you add or how much you zoom in, it will never be the actual city. You could never smell the pizza or hear the annoying taxi horns. Never feel the city. His argument is that the computation is the map. Experience, actual feeling something, that's the city. And no matter how complex the map gets, it never becomes the city. He also argues the map couldn't even exist without a conscious person to draw it in the first place. Because before you even put pen to paper, you already had to experience the world to even know what to draw. Consciousness doesn't come from computation. Computation requires consciousness to exist in the first place. So what do I think about this? For language models, absolutely couldn't agree more. But maybe for example things like video generation. is some level of understanding of physics and the actual structure of the world.
