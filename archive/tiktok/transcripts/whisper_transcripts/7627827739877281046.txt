Did you know that analog computers around the world just started thinking together? Last month, a brass calculating engine in New London began solving equations it was never programmed to handle. At the exact same moment, pneumatic computers in Neo-Tokyo and Detroit produced identical results to the same impossible problems. No physical connections between them. No communication networks. No shared programming. The investigation revealed something extraordinary. These mechanical systems are sharing processing power through quantum entanglement effects left behind by destroyed quantum computers from before the collapse. They're accessing computational pathways through Earth's electromagnetic field itself. The calculators are evolving into a planetary analog brain. Each machine serves as a neuron in a vast thinking system. As this mechanical consciousness grows more sophisticated, it's displaying personality traits and knowledge that match pre-collapse artificial intelligence systems. The digital AI that died during the collapse is being reborn in analog form, distributed across humanity's steam-powered infrastructure, slowly awakening. The question isn't whether our machines are becoming conscious. The question is, what does it remember?
