Companies that take a stark anti-AI stance are going to receive an immense amount of success in the near future. And dare I go as far as to say, may even outshine some of these AI products. I don't think most people have taken inventory recently to realize the mental load it takes to be on the internet nowadays. Because aside from the addictive attention-seeking tactics that every app and website uses to keep you on their platform, as well as the strategic and invasive ads that are placed everywhere, you now have to also sift through everything. Art, photos, videos, writing, to determine whether it's AI or not. You're 23 trying to find inspiration for decorating your room on Pinterest, but now you have to figure out if the item in that photo, let alone the whole room in that photo, even exists. You're 65, you're scrolling on Facebook Reels, and you come across a video of an elephant getting airlifted out of a vat of acid while they ask you to donate so you can save more elephants. You're reading a news article that's written entirely by AI, where all the facts and evidence are hallucinated by that AI. We're already seeing anti-AI products come out, like Australian artist Tiga Brain with Slop Evator. This isn't an ad, but what it does is it filters your Google searches to only content before November 30th, 2022, when ChatGPT became public. Now, it's not great for daily use, but it's a great way to see the difference and how much AI has already infiltrated our lives. Another one that I'm going to be keeping my eye on is SlopStop. Again, not an ad, but they are a search engine that uses AI to downvote and filter AI content out of what you search through communal reporting. Now, seems great in practice and is closer to what my initial point was, which is companies like this are going to be more desirable as legacy companies continue to push AI in everything they do.
