Don't go and upload your images to ChatGPT and other AI image generators just because of a trend. Over the last two years, people have been uploading selfies into artificial intelligence to turn themselves into cartoons, fantasy characters, painted portraits, or even AI avatars. It does look fun, but here's what you're actually risking. Firstly, there's something called metadata. People don't realize that most photos contain hidden data like your location, date, time, and even your device. And if that image is leaked or even reused, that data can be used to track routines, identify your home or even target you with scams. Second, you need to understand your face is biometric data. A selfie isn't just a photo. You can't go change your faces if you're changing a password. And if you go and upload photos of your children or even your family, you're making that decision for them. Those images can be used for deepfakes, impersonation scams, fake content if they're ever leaked or exposed. ChatGPT also has the full right to those images as soon as you upload it. This can be used to train specific models, but also if someone else is using a chat in ChatGPT, your image might actually come up when they're generating something. Third, background leaks. Photos give a lot more than you actually think. Home layouts, uniforms, access badges, and even reflections on screens. Sometimes attackers don't even have to hack you. They just wait for you to post the information yourself. And lastly, control. As soon as you upload an image into a cloud, it's a data handoff. You can't control where it goes, how long it's actually stored, or even what happens when there's a breach and your stuff gets leaked. So when you upload something to ChatGPT, it is no longer yours. They have full rights over it. Uploading your photos to artificial intelligence isn't instant disaster, but there are long-term effects. Think before you upload. A trend only lasts a day, but your data stays there forever. Stay safe and follow for more.
