It's a bloodbath at Disney, and Marvel Studios just took the worst of it. Yesterday, the company laid off approximately 1,000 employees company-wide, and nearly the entire visual development team at Marvel was let go, including people who have been there over a decade, with only a skeleton crew kept on to coordinate outside contractors going forward. These are the artists who built the visual identity of the MCU, the concept designers, the world builders, and Disney just replaced them with the contractor model. The visual effects community saw this coming. For years, workers described an industry in crisis, one that was overworked, underpaid, and horribly micromanaged, with Marvel constantly demanding major changes, sometimes even just weeks before a release, and the on-screen quality reflected it. The Marvels became the lowest-grossing MCU film in history, and Ant-Man and Wasp, Quantumania, massively disappointed. Box office analysts stopped calling Marvel releases sure bets and actually started calling them risk-loaded. And this is the first major restructuring move under new Disney CEO Josh D'Amaro, who took over from Bob Iger just last month. In his memo to staff, D'Amaro said that the cuts were building a more agile and technologically enabled workforce. That phrase is doing a lot of work because the roles that were just eliminated are exactly the ones AI image generation is being positioned to replace. Marvel built the most dominant franchise in cinema history on the backs of those artists. Di Amaro called it agility, but the artists who just lost their jobs after a decade building the MCU probably have a different word for it. As always, we'd love to hear your thoughts.
