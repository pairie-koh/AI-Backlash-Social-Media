What if I told you the U.S. doesn't just invent surveillance tools? It exports them to dictators. After testing them here first, of course. From facial recognition to predictive policing spyware, America leads the world in surveillance tech. But it's not just for national security. Private U.S. companies have sold biometric databases, surveillance drones, and hacking tools to regimes in Saudi Arabia, Egypt, and India. These countries then use them to target journalists, activists, religious minorities, and opposition leaders. And a lot of this tech gets tested first on America's own poor communities, especially minority-heavy neighborhoods. The NYPD ran facial recognition on housing projects years before it became mainstream. ICE used Amazon software to track migrants. And during 2020's protests, DHS deployed aerial drones over U.S. cities without warrants. It's not just government overreach. It's big business. Defense contractors build the tech, lobbyists make sure there's no laws to stop it, and then it's exported like any other product.
