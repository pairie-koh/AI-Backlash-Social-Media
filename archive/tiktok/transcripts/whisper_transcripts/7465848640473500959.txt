Does the energy efficiency of DeepSeek make us more optimistic for the climate future because of its lower resource intensity? This is a question that I wanted to investigate because when I heard about DeepSeek and I saw on this post that it could have a massive efficiency bump on energy efficiency, I kind of thought, well, maybe this is good news for energy and climate. Well, it turns out that we don't know yet. It's kind of murky. And one of those reasons is because if you have higher efficiency AI, we might see an increase in the amount of AI adoption. DeepSeq itself says that its model uses roughly 10 to 40 times less energy than similar AI technologies. Lawrence Berkeley National Lab, which is one of our DOE-funded national labs, predicts that AI-driven data centers could account for 12% of U.S. energy demand by 2028. So that's pretty large. We are obviously seeing that AI is driving a lot of energy demand. And unfortunately, a lot of that energy demand is going to be met by non-renewable energies. I will continue watching this story as someone who is skeptical of AI and its proliferation from a climate perspective.
