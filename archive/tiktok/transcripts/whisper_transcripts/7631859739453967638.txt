Now bring this even closer to something personal. In 2023, in Arizona, a mother received a phone call. She heard her daughter's voice crying, panicked, saying she had been kidnapped. The situation felt real. The emotion felt real. The urgency was real. But the voice was not. Her daughter was safe. The voice had been cloned. And that is where the technology becomes most dangerous. Because in those first few seconds, people don't analyze. They react. And this same pattern is now scaling inside organizations. In February 2024, in Hong Kong, a finance employee joined what appeared to be a routine internal video call. Colleagues were already present, including what looked like senior executives. The conversation felt normal. Questions were answered. Instructions were given. Nothing seemed unusual. The employee followed the instructions. And over the course of that interaction, around $25 million was transferred. Only later did the company realize what had happened. The people on the call were not real. They had been generated using deep fake technology, not a fake message, not a suspicious email, a fully simulated environment that behaved like reality.
